UNESCO Global Geopark · Finland

Step Into a World
That Has No Road

Saimaa is not a lake. It is an inland sea that happens to be freshwater — 1,700 square kilometres of water so clear you can watch the bottom from ten metres up. Formed by glaciers retreating 10,000 years ago, the lake holds the geological memory of a continent.

UNESCO declared it a Global Geopark in 2021 — not for tourism, but because the geological record here is irreplaceable. No car reaches the archipelago interior. No coach tour navigates the narrow granite channels between islands. Some of these islands have never had a permanent human structure built on them.

At 5am on Saimaa the silence is a physical thing. The only sound is the bow cutting water and, sometimes, a loon calling across an empty channel. This is what wilderness means when it is not a marketing word.

Saimaa Ringed Seal · Pusa hispida saimensis

The Jewel Inside
the Wilderness

There are roughly 500 of them left. The Saimaa ringed seal exists nowhere else on Earth. It never adapted to the open ocean. It never will. This lake is its entire world — and the wilderness you are sailing through is its home.

On calm mornings, a grey shape appears on a sun-warmed granite shelf. It rests there in the silence — ears folded, whiskers catching the light — utterly indifferent to the mist rising from the water. You are watching an animal that evolution left in paradise and forgot about.

"Possible natural observation — we cruise seal habitat with expert naturalists on board. A sighting is possible. It is never guaranteed, and should never be. We guarantee you will be where they live."

Your presence here contributes — specifically. A dedicated conservation contribution from every booking funds the Finnish Association for Nature Conservation (FANC) Saimaa Seal LIFE project and Metsähallitus Parks & Wildlife Finland — the two organisations responsible for creating snow drifts for pup-rearing dens and seal-safe fishing gear across the lake. Guests are invited to contribute to an active citizen-science photo-identification study on board.

You are not a spectator. You are part of the story.

Nights 1 & 6 · Outbound & Return
Sahanlahti Resort

Set into a sheltered cove on Puumala island. Low buildings, Finnish birch and pine architecture, every room facing water. The smoke sauna is at the lake's edge — heated since morning when you arrive. Rowing boats and kayaks at the dock.

The kitchen sources from the archipelago and local farms: pike-perch, chanterelles, cloudberries, rye. The cove holds the silence. In the morning the mist sits on the water and you can hear a fish jump from the dock.

Nights 2 & 5 · Outbound & Return
Järvisydän Resort

Järvisydän means lake heart. The ship ties up directly at the long dock. You walk off the gangway into the resort. Green Key certified, built on the principle that the landscape is the architecture.

The gala dinner programme is the centrepiece of the route. European Region of Gastronomy 2024. The chefs work with local producers, foragers, and fishermen on seasonal provenance. The menu changes with the lake — not with the calendar.

The Route · Seven Nights

Seven Nights. One Complete Circle.

Weekly departures every Wednesday at 09:00 from Lappeenranta. Returns the following Tuesday morning — the circle closes.

1 Wednesday

Lappeenranta — Cast Off

Depart Lappeenranta harbour at 09:00. The fortress town falls away within the hour. Brief stop at Imatra (1.5h from departure) — the industrial north, rapids that once powered factories. A harder register that makes the archipelago which follows feel richer by contrast.

Evening navigation brings you into the southern archipelago. The open water gives way to narrow granite channels. Sahanlahti arrival. Smoke sauna at the lake's edge, heated since 3pm. The plunge pool is the lake.

✓ Regional lunch on board · Sahanlahti accommodation (Night 1) · Dinner · Smoke sauna · Expedition team seal programme
2 Thursday

Savonlinna — Olavinlinna by Water

Re-embark at 09:00. The naturalist has been on deck since 06:30. Olavinlinna appears through morning mist before you see the city — the medieval castle sits on a granite island in the middle of the channel. Your historian has been speaking for twenty minutes by the time it appears. Then silence. Then the castle. You pass beneath its walls at eye level with the foundations. 1.5h passage through Savonlinna.

Järvisydän arrival in the afternoon. The ship ties directly at the long dock. Green Key resort, built on the principle that the landscape is the architecture.

✓ Breakfast & lunch on board · Historian castle passage briefing · Järvisydän accommodation (Night 2) · Dinner
3 Friday

Varkaus — The Lock Descent

North through the Varkaus canal. 1h passage through historic industrial locks cut through bedrock. The ship descends through the lock chamber — granite walls close in, water drops, sky narrows to a strip above you, then opens again. Unexpectedly beautiful. Evening talk: Finnish canal engineering and the Saimaa waterway.

Kuopio arrival at the northernmost point of the lake. Night 3 at the city's quayside.

✓ Regional lunch on board · Kuopio accommodation (Night 3) · Dinner

Kuopio Full Day — The Northernmost Point

A full day at the apex of the route. Puijo Tower panorama — 360° view of the lake system that shows how vast the Saimaa watershed truly is. Kuopio market hall, one of Finland's finest indoor markets: smoked fish, fresh berries, kalakukko. At leisure in the afternoon.

Two nights at the northernmost point of the lake. The scale of Saimaa becomes comprehensible here — and more beautiful for it.

Plan Your Voyage

When to Come

August — the peak. Long days, warm water (18–22°C). Seals sunning on rocks in the morning. Resort terraces open until 11pm. The light at 8pm on Saimaa in August is the reason photographers come to Finland.

September — the choice of those who know. The crowds are gone. The forest starts turning — birch yellow, rowan red, against the dark pines. The water is still warm enough to swim. Seal sightings are excellent; the animals are more active before winter. The gala dinner in September, with the red birch behind the terrace, is something else entirely.

Packing: August 18–24°C days, 12–15°C evenings. September mornings are crisp — bring a fleece and light waterproof. The ship has covered deck space for all weather.

Getting There

  • By Train2.5 hours from Helsinki Central. Direct, comfortable, scenic. Recommended.
  • By Coach3 hours. Multiple daily departures from Helsinki.
  • By AirHEL → LPR, 45 minutes. Finnair seasonal schedule.
  • TransferPrivate transfer from Helsinki or HEL bookable via FinnConcierge.

Most guests arrive the evening before and spend a night in Lappeenranta. The fortress and harbour restaurants are worth the full evening.

Helsinki-Vantaa (HEL) is the main international gateway with direct flights from DACH and UK cities.

Conservation · Sustainability · About Us

The Arctic Cruises Difference

Arctic Cruises is built around a single conviction: that luxury travel and genuine conservation are not contradictions. They require each other.

Conservation — Named and Funded

A dedicated conservation contribution from every booking funds the Finnish Association for Nature Conservation (FANC) Saimaa Seal LIFE project and Metsähallitus Parks & Wildlife Finland — the two organisations responsible for the survival of Pusa hispida saimensis. Their work: artificial snow drifts for pup-rearing dens, seal-safe fishing gear replacement, and annual population monitoring. We publish an impact report each January. Our guests have access to it before, during, and after their cruise.

Sustainability Built In

The route operates within UNESCO Global Geopark Saimaa — strict environmental protocols apply to all vessel operations, including designated no-wake zones near seal habitats and zero-discharge waste management. Sahanlahti Resort: regional sourcing, traditional smoke sauna, minimal-footprint architecture. Järvisydän Resort: Green Key certified, European Region of Gastronomy 2024, all gala ingredients within 30km. FinnConcierge is designed to reduce paper waste and optimise guest schedules to minimise transport.

About Arctic Cruises

Arctic Cruises is a Finnish tourism company founded by Patrick Heiskanen and Finland DMC Oy, purpose-built for multi-night expedition cruising on Lake Saimaa. We operate in partnership with Järvisydän Oy on the southern route and are the exclusive expedition cruise operator in the Saimaa UNESCO Global Geopark. The 2027 season is our inaugural international season. The 2026 FAM inauguration sailed in September 2026.
