### A) Gaps Per Document

**1. FAM Trip Itinerary**
*   **Gap 1 (Commercial Relevance):** The FAM is in September 2026 for a high-summer (August 2027) product. The document fails to address the experiential difference (daylight hours, temperature, foliage, general "feel"). A product manager will question the validity of a shoulder-season FAM for a peak-season product. This needs a single sentence acknowledging the difference and explaining the timing (e.g., "Note: This FAM showcases the route in the clear autumn season; the commercial product operates in August for peak summer conditions and maximum daylight.").
*   **Gap 2 (B2B Focus):** The "What Is Included" table's "Paid by" column is internal information (DMOs, Lappeenranta City). A product manager does not care who funds the FAM; they care what is included for their future client. This column creates noise and should be removed. The focus must be on the commercial product's inclusions/exclusions.

**2. M/S Carelia — FAM Trip Ship Programme**
*   **Gap 1 (Sales Funnel Friction):** The call to action on Slide 11 is to "Apply for the Arctic Cruises 2027 Operator Programme." "Applying" creates a psychological barrier; it implies scarcity and potential rejection after you have invested four days of their time. This should be reframed as "Register for the 2027 Operator Programme" or "Activate Your Partnership." The goal is to onboard partners, not to create a selection process.
*   **Gap 2 (Operational Reality):** The self-service booking model (Slide 8) is described as "no human contact needed." This is a red flag. A product manager needs to know the process for modifications, cancellations, group deviations, and special requests. The claim is unrealistic and undermines trust. It must be clarified to "Self-service portal for standard bookings, with a dedicated agent contact for modifications and support."

**3. Invitation**
*   **Gap 1 (Route Definition):** The "Full circuit" description is contradictory and nonsensical. It lists a 13-day out-and-back journey (`LPR → Kuopio → (return) → Järvisydän → LPR`) which conflicts with the "7 days / 6 nights" headline and the "7-day loop" described in the Ship Programme presentation. This is a critical error in the core product definition.
*   **Gap 2 (Schedule Clarity):** The "18 departure days" logic ("3 weeks × 6 boarding points") is presented as a math problem. A product manager needs a simple, clear schedule. Rephrase it, e.g., "Daily departures for three consecutive weeks in August, with a different port serving as the primary departure point each day of the week."
