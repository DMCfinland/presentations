### 1. EU AI Act Attack

Your reliance on "advisory only + human review" for compliance is naive and dangerous. The EU AI Act explicitly lists AI systems used for recruitment or selection, particularly for "evaluating candidates," as high-risk in Annex III, regardless of whether a human makes the final decision. A Finnish regulator would argue that `ai_filter v3`'s core function of scoring candidate "fit_score 1-5" directly constitutes evaluation, bringing it squarely under high-risk classification. The human in the loop merely adds a layer of mitigation; it doesn't change the system's inherent high-risk nature.

**Specific Risks:**
1.  **Functional Misclassification:** The regulator could deem your "advisory" system functionally high-risk if statistical analysis shows human decisions align too closely with AI recommendations or if human overrides are infrequent, implying the AI is effectively driving outcomes.
2.  **Immediate Documentation Demand:** Instead of "adding by Aug 2026," a high-risk classification triggers immediate, stringent requirements for a conformity assessment, robust risk management systems, human oversight measures, detailed technical documentation, and post-market monitoring from deployment.
**Recommendation:** Immediately commission a formal, independent legal opinion specifically on the high-risk classification of `ai_filter v3` under the current EU AI Act text, focusing on its candidate evaluation function, to understand full obligations now.

**Specific Risks:**

2.  **"Badge of Shame" Perception:** If the attempts to penetrate the "gatekeeper" via obscure 2nd-degree connections are clumsy or poorly executed, it can flag Riikka as an overly aggressive or desperate recruiter, harming her professional reputation in the tightly-knit Finnish executive circles.

### 3. Finnish Market Attack

Your assumptions about the Finnish executive market are dangerously generalized. "Passive but accessible" and "direct and brief" are vague platitudes that ignore critical nuances. Finnish executives are often reserved, valuing established trust and long-term relationships over opportunistic, unsolicited approaches. An aggressive "direct" headhunter outreach, without a clear, pre-existing reputation or highly compelling offer, is more likely to be perceived as intrusive and unprofessional, leading to a silent dismissal or even reputational backlash, particularly within conservative sectors.

**Specific Risks:**
1.  **Reputational Backfire in Specific Sectors:** A direct approach to executives in Finland's public sector, cooperative companies, or family firms (where legacy, trust, and internal networks are paramount) can be seen as highly inappropriate, generating negative word-of-mouth that quickly spreads.
2.  **Telegram Channel Mismatch:** Assuming Telegram is a valid B2B executive communication channel in Finland is fundamentally misguided. It's predominantly a personal messaging app; professional outreach via Telegram will likely be ignored as unprofessional, spam, or even suspicious, significantly lowering response rates.
**Recommendation:** Conduct urgent, targeted qualitative interviews with 5-10 active Finnish executives and 2-3 executive gatekeepers across your target sectors to validate (or invalidate) specific communication preferences, acceptable outreach styles, and perceived professionalism of channels like Telegram.

### 4. AI Personalization Attack

The claim of "2-3x response rate" or "287% omnichannel improvement" is generic marketing hype, entirely unsupported for high-stakes executive headhunting. Senior executives are masters at identifying generic, templated, or thinly veiled AI-generated content. An "AI-drafted + human-reviewed" message, if it still carries the subtle hallmarks of AI (e.g., overly polite, slightly off-tone, lacking genuine spontaneity), will be immediately perceived as "AI-generated." This perception triggers distrust, a feeling of being mass-marketed to, and a rapid decline in response rates because it signals a lack of genuine, bespoke effort and respect for their time.

**Specific Risks:**
1.  **"Uncanny Valley" in Outreach:** AI-assisted personalization, when not perfect, can create messages that feel almost right but slightly synthetic – an "uncanny valley" effect that actively alienates recipients more than purely human or even purely generic outreach.
2.  **Executive Fatigue with AI Spam:** As AI tools become more prevalent, executives will develop an even keener sense for AI-generated messages. Being an early adopter of poorly disguised AI outreach could preemptively categorize Riikka's messages as low-effort spam.
**Recommendation:** Discard all generic claims. Implement rigorous A/B testing: human-only drafted outreach vs. AI-drafted + human-reviewed, specifically for *response rates and qualitative feedback from executives* on perceived authenticity and personalization. Be prepared to significantly scale back AI use if it fails to surpass human performance in this critical metric.

### 5. Commission Structure Attack

Assuming a solo recruiter, even with AI, can command 25-33% retained search fees (€25-50k per placement) is highly unrealistic for a "Wave 2" validation. These premium rates are typically reserved for established executive search *firms* with significant brand reputation, extensive researcher teams, and robust indemnification. A solo operator, lacking the firm's infrastructure and established trust, will almost certainly be viewed as a contingency recruiter. Contingency fees are lower (15-20%) and only paid upon success, fundamentally undermining your financial projections and requiring higher volume or higher value placements to achieve profitability.

**Specific Risks:**
1.  **Client Resistance to Retained Model:** Potential clients will question the value proposition of paying upfront retained fees to an individual without the institutional guarantees and extensive network breadth of a traditional firm, regardless of AI tools.
2.  **Cash Flow Catastrophe:** If the market dictates contingency fees, your initial 1-deal goal becomes financially unviable. The significant customer acquisition cost (CAC) of building this AI system would be utterly disproportionate to the expected, delayed revenue, leading to immediate cash flow problems.
**Recommendation:** Immediately conduct primary market research on prevailing fee structures for *independent, solo executive recruiters* in Finland, clearly distinguishing between retained and contingency models. Adjust your financial model and value proposition to reflect the actual market rates an individual can command.
