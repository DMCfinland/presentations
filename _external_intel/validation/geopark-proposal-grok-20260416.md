1. KILLER WEAKNESSES (what will make Mikko say no)
Mikko’s actual likely objections as Executive Director of a small-staffed UNESCO Global Geopark (only ~2–3 core people plus a geologist) hosting the 18th EGN Conference (16–18 Sept 2026, not 17–19) are highly specific and reputation-driven:

UNESCO brand & scientific accuracy risk: Geoparks live or die by the credibility of their geological storytelling. Any AI hallucination on rock ages, formation processes, or protected sites would be catastrophic for a network that undergoes UNESCO revalidation every four years. Mikko knows Generative AI still hallucinates on niche geo-heritage; he will not risk the Geopark’s UNESCO label on a pilot built by a tourism DMC.
Commercial perception & “hidden agenda”: The proposal explicitly lists “Arctic Cruises bookings pre/post-conference” as a DMC benefit. Mikko will instantly read this as a sales funnel wearing a Geopark badge. EGN/UNESCO partnerships must demonstrably serve geoconservation, education, and sustainable local development — not funnel international delegates to a DMC’s cruise product. Their existing “Saimaa Geopark Partner” program is free but strictly non-commercial in spirit.
Data privacy / GDPR + international delegates: 500 participants from New Zealand, Africa, all of Europe. Every chat query (family programs, restaurants, field trips) creates personal data. UNESCO has its own AI ethics recommendation; Mikko will demand iron-clad DPA, data ownership by the Geopark, deletion policy, and proof of GDPR compliance before he even forwards it to the board. Approval process is not “director decides in 20 min”: EGN rules and typical geopark practice require formal partnership agreements, alignment with the management plan, and often Coordination Committee or municipal stakeholder sign-off. Saimaa has nine municipalities behind it. A cold “free pilot” will be escalated, not green-lit in May.
