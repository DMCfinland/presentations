# Research Synthesis — Yacht Fractional Sales Presentation (S178)

## Slide Structure
1. **Lead with aspiration, anchor with logic — in that order.** Open emotional hook (lifestyle image + one sentence), then financial validation. If you open with numbers, Nordic buyers treat it as a product spec, not a lifestyle decision.
2. **"Problem with Full Ownership" slide missing — high-converter.** Show the pain first: full owner pays 499k€ and uses it 3-5 weeks/year while bearing 100% of costs. Pre-frames 125k€ buy-in as obvious solution.
3. **Kill the timeshare objection with its own dedicated slide.** SeaNet/YATCO flag this as #1 conversion blocker. "Fractional ownership = true legal equity, not usage rights" — must be stated explicitly for Finnish 40-55yo legal/finance backgrounds.
4. **Exit strategy belongs in the deck, not in FAQ.** Buyers who ask feel you're hiding something. Programs addressing it proactively (minimum hold period, buyback, co-owner right of first refusal) convert better.

## Hero Photo Strategy
5. **Lifestyle shot wins as lead image; exterior profile wins in gallery.** On-water running/action shot as hero, detailed exterior profile as supporting. Current bow shot is borderline — if it's a static dock shot, replace with yacht underway in blue Mediterranean water.
6. **Golden hour lighting is not optional at this price point.** "Viewers automatically go to the lightest part of the image." Mediterranean afternoon running shot = 10x more impact than harbor dock shot.
7. **Combine: lifestyle hero + interior warmth + technical exterior profile.** Lifestyle → aspiration; interior → emotional close; exterior profile → rational buyer satisfaction.

## Top Objections
8. **Scheduling fairness = #1 operational objection.** Nordic buyers are precise. Top programs use rotating draft-style priority system (Owner 1 picks first in Year 1, rotates). One guaranteed major holiday per owner per year. Must specify the mechanism, not just "12 weeks guaranteed."
9. **Depreciation framing is backward in most decks.** Reframe: "You bear only 1/4 of any depreciation, while professional maintenance keeps vessel in charter-grade condition year-round." Then pivot to charter revenue offsetting costs.
10. **"Loss of control" objection is real for Nordic HNWIs.** Acknowledge + reframe: "You gain access to a 499k€ asset and professional management team; you trade individual decision-making for guaranteed standards."

## Financial Model Credibility
11. **Charter comparison as price anchor = strongest credibility move.** Chartering comparable 60-foot in Puerto Banús: €20,000–35,000/week. Break-even vs. chartering at 6-8 owned weeks = 4-5 years. Make the break-even year EXPLICIT (e.g., "By Year 4, you've passed charter cost parity").
12. **Show management entity and legal structure explicitly.** Name the holding entity, jurisdiction, mechanism. Vague "you own a share" = trust killer for Nordic HNWIs.
13. **Bear case must be genuinely conservative.** If Bear still shows positive return, sophisticated Nordic buyers will discount all three scenarios. Show a realistic Bear (2-3 charter weeks, small loss or breakeven). Builds more trust than 3 optimistic scenarios.

## Surprising Findings
14. **Transparency about illiquidity converts BETTER than hiding it.** Buyers who hear "capital likely locked for 4-6 years" self-select as serious; their conversion rate is far higher than buyers who discover this late.
15. **Tax angle underused in European fractional decks.** VAT recovery on charter operations, Finnish EVL depreciation rules for business assets. A single half-slide "speak to your tax advisor about VAT recovery and depreciation structures" signals sophistication to Finnish 40-55yo finance professionals.
