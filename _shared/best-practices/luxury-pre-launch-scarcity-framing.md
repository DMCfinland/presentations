On pre-launch luxury products (no bookings yet), artificial urgency tactics ("High Demand", "Selling Fast", countdown timers) destroy the trust that luxury brands depend on. The 50+ HNWI audience detects manufactured scarcity immediately.

**Why:** Luxury buyers are sophisticated and experienced. They know a product launching in 2027 with no bookings open cannot have "High Demand" in April 2026. When they detect the manipulation, they don't just ignore it — they downgrade the entire brand credibility. Grok: "fake scarcity backfires in premium travel." Gemini: "It signals desperation, not desirability. Attracts wrong customer, repels right one."

**The right move:** Use genuine historical or category scarcity:
- Historical scarcity: "Route not operated for approximately 50 years — 2027 is the inaugural international season."
- Structural scarcity: "Maximum 100 guests per departure — by design." (honest, factual, luxury-appropriate)
- Category scarcity: "The only cruise through the Saimaa UNESCO Global Geopark." (genuine, not manufactured)

**Patrick caught this before the spar confirmed it** — S239 correction before Grok/Gemini debrief. Fast-tracked to Tier A.

**How to apply:**
- Before adding any urgency signal to a luxury product page, ask: "Is this literally true right now?"
- If the answer is no → reframe as historical, structural, or category scarcity
- Applies to: pre-launch sites, new product announcements, inaugural season positioning
