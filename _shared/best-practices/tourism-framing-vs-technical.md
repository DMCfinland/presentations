# Tourism Framing vs Technical Framing

**Source:** Patrick, session 82 (photo mining) + session ~70 (map building)
**Tier:** B → fast-track to A (Patrick correction, 2 confirmed failure modes)
**Confidence:** 0.7

---

## The Pattern

Claude defaults to **technical/professional framing** when searching, building, or describing things.
In tourism, this ALWAYS produces wrong results. Tourism sells **emotion, aspiration, and experience** — not technical correctness.

### Two confirmed failure modes (both source: patrick):
1. **Photo search:** Searching "XC ski Finland frozen lake" → returns competitive athletes in lycra on race tracks. Should be "people enjoying winter freedom, scenic frozen lake Finland."
2. **Map building:** Claude insisted on mathematically/geographically correct proportions → tourism maps must look beautiful and inviting, not accurate. Accuracy kills the dream.

---

## The Norway Mental Model

Think like **Visit Norway** or **Visit Finland** marketers:
- They never say "cross-country skiing" — they say "glide through silence on a frozen lake"
- They never show correct scale maps — they show illustrated journey maps with warm colors
- They never describe terrain — they describe how it **feels**

**Before searching/building anything for tourism:** ask "how would Visit Norway headline this?"

## Map Building: Tourism Rules

- **Correct geography = wrong for tourism.** Maps should feel inviting, not accurate.
- Use illustrated/watercolor style maps, not topographic or satellite
- Exaggerate landmark sizes (hotel looks bigger than a village — that's correct for tourism)
- Show journey routes, not borders
- Warm colors, not cold cartographic blues
- See: `lapland-map-vincent.webp` in the Lapland deck — this is the standard

---

## General Rule for All Tourism Deliverables

Before any search, build, or description in a tourism context:

1. **Strip the technical noun** — remove sport, geography, science terms
2. **Replace with experience/emotion noun** — how does it feel, what do you see, what do you remember
3. **Add human element** — who is there, what are they wearing, what's the mood
4. **Add sensory detail** — silence, light, cold air, vast horizon

This applies to: photo searches, map designs, slide copy, itinerary descriptions, email subject lines.
