## DOCUMENT STRUCTURE

### PART 1: THE DESTINATION — LAKE SAIMAA

**1.1 Geography**
- Location: Eastern Finland, South Karelia + North Karelia + South Savo regions
- Size: 4,400 km² total lake system (1,700 km² main lake), ~4,400 islands and islets
- Formation: Glacial retreat 10,000 years ago — moraine ridges created the island system
- Water quality: Meets drinking water standards (freshwater, filtered through ancient granite)
- Connection to sea: None — landlocked, entirely freshwater
- Temperature: 18-24°C in summer (warm enough to swim comfortably)

**1.2 UNESCO Global Geopark Status**
- Designation: UNESCO Global Geopark, granted 2021
- What it means: Recognition of outstanding geology AND human cultural connection to landscape
- Significance: One of only ~180 UNESCO Geoparks worldwide; the only one centred on a major Finnish lake system
- Implications for operators: UNESCO recognition = established credibility for trade marketing

**1.3 The Wildlife**
- Saimaa ringed seal (*Pusa hispida saimensis*): ~500 individuals, endemic to Lake Saimaa, found NOWHERE else on Earth
- History: Trapped in the lake when glaciers retreated 8,000+ years ago; evolved as separate subspecies
- Threats: Climate change (reduced snow cover → fewer pupping dens), bycatch in fishing nets
- Current conservation: FANC Saimaa Seal LIFE project — artificial snow drifts, seal-safe fishing gear, annual population monitoring
- Viewing ethics: "Possible natural observation" — never promise, never approach, never disturb
- Other wildlife: Osprey, white-tailed eagle, common crane, moose (on shore), pike-perch, zander, bream

**1.4 The Landscape**
- Islands: Virtually unbuilt — many have never had a permanent human structure
- Shores: Granite bedrock, ancient forest (pine, birch, spruce), minimal visible infrastructure
- The silence: No roads through the archipelago interior. No car can reach most of these islands.
- Light: Summer sun sets ~10pm in August, ~8:30pm in September. Extraordinary low light.

**1.5 Human History**
- 12,000 years of continuous habitation around Lake Saimaa
- Karelian culture: Eastern Finland has distinct cultural identity — different from Western Finnish coast
- Trade routes: Saimaa was the main highway for 600+ years (before roads) — goods moved by water
- Olavinlinna Castle (Savonlinna): Built 1475, one of the best-preserved medieval castles in Northern Europe, built on a granite island in the main shipping channel
- Finnish sauna: The smoke sauna (*savusauna*) is the original form — heated 6-8 hours, no chimney, charred walls. Sahanlahti and Järvisydän both have authentic smoke saunas.
