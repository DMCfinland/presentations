## 3. Strategic Engine

**How This Actually Works:**
AI treats Excel files as structured objects with queryable components (sheets, columns, formulas, formatting). By providing detailed upfront context (current state, desired output, transformation rules), the AI can execute complex multi-step transformations that would take humans hours or days. The key is modular prompting: breaking work into discrete deliverables (clean data → categorized data → revenue model → P&L rollup) to avoid context window failures.

**Key Components:**
1. **Upfront Data Collection:** Gather all inputs before prompting (reverses traditional iterative workflow)
2. **Modular Prompt Architecture:** Break complex tasks into sequential steps with named deliverables (e.g., "return clean data in sheet called 'Clean Data'")
3. **Declarative Intent:** Specify current state, desired state, transformation rules, and validation criteria
4. **Quality Gates:** Build validation checks (reconciliation to source data, error-free formulas, flagged ambiguities)
5. **Format Specifications:** Define professional output (bold headers, subtotals, board-ready formatting)

**Why This Works:**
LLMs excel at pattern recognition and systematic transformation but struggle with ambiguity and context loss. By providing comprehensive briefings and breaking work into checkpoints, you leverage AI's strengths (speed, consistency, formula fluency) while guarding against weaknesses (hallucination, context overflow). The modular approach also enables iterative refinement without re-running entire workflows.

## 4. Behavioral Design (adapted from Culture & Incentives)

**Behavioral Principles:**
1. **Front-load thinking, back-load execution:** Invest time upfront in defining the problem; let AI handle mechanical execution
2. **Declare, don't iterate:** Traditional Excel work involves coffee-break iterations ("I'll pick this up from Daryl's desk"). AI requires batch-mode thinking
3. **Explicit over implicit:** AI needs current-state descriptions, not assumptions. If there's ambiguity, specify tiebreakers or flag for human review

**Incentive Structure:**
- **Encourages:** Systematic thinking, documentation of business logic, reusable templates
- **Discourages:** Ad-hoc manual adjustments, undocumented assumptions, "tribal knowledge" workflows
- **Rewards:** Time savings compound—monthly P&L that took "hours and hours" now takes "5-15 minutes"

**Alignment Mechanisms:**
The speaker emphasizes validation layers: "add a validation section at the bottom" with checklists proving the AI's work. This creates a feedback loop where users learn to write better prompts by reviewing AI output quality. Over time, users internalize what good briefings look like.

## 9. System Health Metric (adapted from North Star Metric)

**What to Optimize For:**
**Time-to-First-Draft:** The elapsed time from "I need a financial model" to "here's a reviewable first draft." 

The speaker's implicit metric: tasks that took "8 days with a team of three" should now take "60 minutes." The goal is to collapse cycle time by orders of magnitude while maintaining quality above 90% threshold (where human review catches the remaining errors).

**Why This Metric:**
Time-to-first-draft captures both AI capability and human prompt quality. If drafts are fast but terrible, the metric reveals poor prompting. If drafts are slow, it reveals workflow friction (poor data collection, context window issues, tool limitations). This metric also compounds—faster drafts enable more iterations, better learning, and ultimately better strategic decisions.

**How to Measure:**
1. **Baseline Historical Time:** Document current time spent on recurring tasks (monthly P&L, board deck, etc.)
2. **AI-Assisted Time:** Track end-to-end time including data collection, prompting, and validation
3. **Quality Gate:** Only count as "first draft" if output requires <20% rework
4. **Iteration Tracking:** Monitor how many prompt attempts needed (goal: trend toward one-shot success)

## 11. Application & Mental Model

### When to Use This Pattern

**Applicable Conditions:**
- **Recurring Financial Tasks:** Monthly/quarterly reports, board decks, forecasts
- **Data Already Digitized:** You have CSVs, exports, or structured data (even if messy)
- **Standardizable Logic:** The transformation rules can be articulated (even if complex)
- **Time-Intensive Manual Work:** Tasks currently taking hours/days of human time
- **Professional Stakes:** Output quality matters (investor decks, audit trails)

**Signal Indicators:**
- You're paying analysts to do repetitive Excel work
- You're delaying strategic decisions waiting for financial models
- Your team says "I wish we had time to build that analysis"
- You're recreating similar spreadsheets from scratch repeatedly
- Excel files are described as "only Sarah knows how this works"

### When NOT to Use This Pattern

**Backfire Conditions:**
- **No Digital Data:** If inputs are handwritten notes or photos of whiteboards, OCR quality becomes the bottleneck
- **Ambiguous Requirements:** If you can't articulate what "clean" or "correct" looks like, AI will guess wrong
- **Irreducible Human Judgment:** Some analyses require contextual knowledge that can't be specified in prompts (e.g., "is this vendor trustworthy?")
- **Regulatory Compliance Needs:** If you need auditable human-in-the-loop for SOX compliance, pure AI execution may not meet requirements
- **One-Time Tasks:** If you'll never use the prompt again, the upfront investment may not pay off vs. manual work

**Inappropriate Scenarios:**
- Early-stage startups with zero financial infrastructure (need to build systems first)
- Analyses requiring proprietary industry knowledge AI doesn't have
- Situations where "showing your work" legally requires human-executed calculations
- Organizations culturally resistant to AI (adoption costs exceed productivity gains)
