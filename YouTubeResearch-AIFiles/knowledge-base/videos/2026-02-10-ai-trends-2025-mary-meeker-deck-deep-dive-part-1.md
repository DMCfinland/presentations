# AI Trends 2025: Mary Meeker Deck Deep Dive Part 1

## Summary

This is a strategic walkthrough of Mary Meeker's 2025 AI Trends deck (her first major deck since 2019's Internet Trends). The core insight: **AI adoption is following internet patterns but 10x faster, driven by hardware breakthroughs from 2017-2018 (Nvidia Volta) that took 5-7 years to fully manifest in market adoption.** The real story isn't just "up and to the right" growth—it's the convergence of three forces: (1) training costs exploding while (2) inference costs collapsing 99.7% in 2 years, creating (3) a proliferated multi-model world where competitive advantages erode quickly. The strategic implication: early infrastructure investments (2019 crypto/ML era) are now paying dividends, and we're still in the first inning of a hardware-enabled revolution where unit economics improve 50,000x while total energy consumption still rises.

## 1. Context

**Background:** 
Mary Meeker, legendary tech investor and former Morgan Stanley analyst (famous for Internet Trends reports), released her first comprehensive deck since 2019—a 340-slide analysis of AI trends. The presenter (Nate) conducts a detailed walkthrough of the first ~143 slides, covering foundational AI metrics, adoption patterns, infrastructure buildout, and cost economics. Meeker has been focused on investing since 2019, making this return to public analysis significant.

**Why This Matters:** 
This represents the definitive "state of AI" briefing from someone who called the internet boom correctly. For business leaders, this is essential intelligence on: (1) whether AI hype is justified (verdict: yes, but nuanced), (2) where infrastructure investments are flowing ($100B+ data center capex), (3) how quickly costs are falling (making AI accessible), and (4) what the competitive landscape looks like (proliferation, not winner-take-all).

## 2. Vision & Why

**Core Mission:** 
Mary Meeker's mission with this deck is to "connect several disparate data points" to make sense of the AI revolution—to set the narrative for how AI is fundamentally different from previous tech waves while also being comparable to the internet in its transformative potential. She positions this as a "meta-technology" that enables optimization across all other domains.

**The "Why" Behind It:**
The underlying driver is that **AI represents a phase shift in capability that will reshape how work gets done, how capital is deployed, and how leadership is defined.** Unlike previous tech transitions, AI is being adopted 10x faster because: (1) broadband internet is already globally saturated, (2) the form factor (text-first chat interface) works on existing devices, and (3) the utility is immediately apparent (unlike early internet which required explanation). The urgency comes from seeing clear patterns in the data: inflection points in 2019 (pre-ChatGPT) in GPU development, ML investments, and data center buildout that took years to manifest publicly.

**Enduring Nature:**

*Timeless principles:*
- Hardware innovation precedes software adoption by 5-7 years (Volta 2017 → ChatGPT boom 2022-23)
- Cost deflation drives mass adoption (light bulb took 75 years for 99% cost reduction; ChatGPT took 2 years)
- Infrastructure buildout happens in waves, often ahead of clear use cases
- Unit economics matter more than hype (50,000x efficiency gains enable scale)
- Proliferation beats concentration in distributed technology systems

*2024-2026 specific:*
- Current model training costs ($100B+) and whether scaling continues
- Specific GPU generations (Volta → Hopper → Blackwell)
- ChatGPT's dominance (likely temporary as models converge)
- Current data center locations and partnerships
- Energy grid constraints as limiting factor

## 3. Strategic Engine

**How This Actually Works:**

The AI revolution operates on a three-layer engine:

1. **Hardware Layer (Foundation):** GPU innovation creates step-function improvements in energy efficiency and compute capability. Nvidia's Volta architecture (2017-2018) reduced energy per token dramatically, making large-scale AI economically viable.

2. **Infrastructure Layer (Enabler):** Massive capex investments ($100B+ annually from big tech) build data centers globally. This happens *before* clear monetization, driven by competitive positioning and belief in future utility.

3. **Application Layer (Monetization):** Once infrastructure exists and costs fall 99%+, rapid user adoption occurs (ChatGPT: 800M users in 2 years vs. Google taking 11 years to reach similar scale). Usage patterns reveal utility, driving more investment in layers 1-2.

The critical insight: **Each layer operates on different timescales.** Hardware innovation (5-7 year cycles) → Infrastructure buildout (2-3 year lag) → Mass adoption (6-12 months once costs hit threshold). This temporal mismatch explains why the 2019 inflection point (visible in developer growth, ML patents, capex) didn't become public-facing until 2022-23.

**Key Components:**

1. **Developer Ecosystem Growth** (leading indicator): 6x growth in Nvidia ecosystem developers signals future application development. This preceded ChatGPT launch by years.

2. **Cost Structure Inversion:** Training costs exploding (enables better models) while inference costs collapse (enables mass distribution). This creates a barbell: huge R&D investments concentrated in few players, but cheap deployment accessible to everyone.

3. **Multi-Cloud Proliferation:** AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, Oracle Cloud, IBM Cloud all scaling. Geographic distribution (US, Europe, Middle East, Asia) prevents single-point concentration. OpenAI's ChatGPT will be "all over the place" in sovereign data centers.

4. **Energy Efficiency Paradox:** Despite 50,000x efficiency improvements, total energy consumption still rising because scale increases faster than efficiency. This creates both opportunity (nuclear revival) and constraint (grid capacity limits growth rate).

5. **Convergence Dynamics:** Model performance converging across providers as techniques proliferate. This suggests competitive advantage lies in distribution, integration, and vertical applications—not pure model capability.

**Why This Works:**

This engine works because of **compounding infrastructure effects**. Each marginal dollar of capex makes the next dollar more productive:
- More data centers → Lower latency → Better user experience → More users → More revenue → More capex
- Cheaper inference → More experimentation → Better applications → More value demonstration → More enterprise adoption → More inference demand
- Developer tools improve → Faster application development → More diverse use cases → More data on what works → Better developer tools

The fundamental unlock is that **hardware innovation (Volta) changed unit economics enough to justify massive infrastructure bets**, which then enables the application explosion we're seeing now. The lag between hardware breakthrough (2017) and public awareness (2022) is the strategic window that separated winners from followers.

## 4. Behavioral Design

**Behavioral Principles:**

1. **Immediate Utility Bias:** ChatGPT succeeded because utility was obvious within 30 seconds of first use. Compare to early internet which required explanation of "what you could do with it." AI adoption accelerates when use cases are self-evident.

2. **Ambient Integration:** The most successful AI implementations (11 Labs audio, customer service bots, search augmentation) operate "ambient"—users benefit without explicit AI interaction. The goal is capability enhancement, not AI-as-feature.

3. **Cost Asymmetry Exploitation:** Users pay subscription fees ($20/month ChatGPT Plus) while inference costs collapse. This creates massive margin potential IF serving costs continue declining faster than price competition drives prices down.

4. **Retention Through Habituation:** The metric that matters is "minutes per day" not just MAUs. ChatGPT capturing 20 minutes daily from millions of users indicates habit formation comparable to social media—but for productive tasks.

**Incentive Structure:**

*The system encourages:*
- **Experimentation over perfection:** Rapidly declining costs mean trying AI is nearly free. This drives exploration of use cases.
- **Infrastructure investment over application development:** Big tech companies pouring $100B+ into data centers before monetization is clear. The bet is that "if you build it, applications will come."
- **Open-sourcing and technique sharing:** Models converging because techniques proliferate. This encourages ecosystem growth over proprietary moats.

*The system discourages:*
- **Waiting for perfect use cases:** The cost of delay (missing infrastructure buildout phase) exceeds the cost of premature investment.
- **Purely cost-focused decisions:** CIOs/CEOs saying they're willing to move to cloud for AI despite cost concerns shows non-price factors (capability, competitive pressure) dominating.

**Alignment Mechanisms:**

The market self-corrects through:
1. **Developer migration:** Developers flow to platforms with best tools/economics. Nvidia's 6x ecosystem growth shows gravitational effects.
2. **User retention metrics:** High retention (approaching Google Search levels) validates utility and justifies continued investment.
3. **Energy constraints:** Physical limits (power grid capacity) naturally throttle growth rate, preventing unsustainable bubbles.

## 5. Time & Attention

**Where Time Flows:**

In this AI infrastructure buildout phase, time/attention allocates to:

1. **Capex Deployment (40%):** Big tech dedicating enormous management attention to data center strategy, chip procurement, energy partnerships. This is "boring" infrastructure work that doesn't generate headlines but determines future optionality.

2. **Efficiency Optimization (30%):** Every marginal improvement in tokens-per-watt or cost-per-token compounds across billions of inferences. Enormous attention to algorithmic efficiency, model compression, serving optimization.

3. **Use Case Discovery (20%):** Despite hype, still early in understanding what AI is actually good for. Time spent on experimentation, A/B testing, user research to identify high-value applications.

4. **Competitive Positioning (10%):** Despite convergence trends, companies still investing in differentiation through vertical integration, exclusive data, custom hardware (Google TPUs, Apple Silicon ML cores).

**What This System DOESN'T Spend On:**

Notably absent or minimized:
- **Regulatory compliance:** While mentioned, not yet a major time sink (will change)
- **Perfect use cases:** The bias is toward deployment and learning vs. planning
- **Legacy system migration:** Less "rip and replace" and more "augment and enhance"
- **Marketing/awareness:** ChatGPT hit 800M users with minimal traditional marketing—product-led growth dominates

The presenter specifically calls out that Meeker avoids deep discussion of:
- Adaptive learning in the wild (how models improve post-deployment)
- Context handling and memory (critical for real applications)
- Intent understanding over time (key for agents)
- The gap between current capabilities and truly autonomous systems

**Allocation Philosophy:**

The underlying principle: **"Hardware enables, applications discover."** 

Time allocation follows a barbell:
- **Heavy investment in infrastructure** (data centers, chips, energy) where scale economies and moats exist
- **Light, experimental investment in applications** where rapid iteration and learning beats planning

This explains why big tech can spend $100B+ on AI capex without clear ROI—they're buying optionality and competitive positioning. The application layer will sort itself out once infrastructure enables cheap experimentation.

## 6. Moats & Time Horizon

**Competitive Advantages:**

1. **Infrastructure Capital Moat (Strongest):**
   - Building data centers requires $billions and 2-3 years
   - Nvidia's CUDA ecosystem creates lock-in (6x developer growth)
   - Energy partnerships (nuclear, grid capacity) not replicable quickly
   - First-mover advantage in geographic locations (sovereign AI considerations)

2. **Data Flywheel Moat (Strong, but eroding):**
   - More users → More usage data → Better models → More users
   - BUT: Technique proliferation means model performance converging
   - AND: Pre-training data limits mean everyone hitting same ceiling
   - SO: Data moat weaker than in previous tech cycles

3. **Distribution Moat (Emerging):**
   - Microsoft's investment in OpenAI gave Azure competitive advantage
   - Google's integration of Gemini into Workspace creates switching costs
   - Apple's on-device AI leverages hardware control
   - These are VERTICAL integration plays, not horizontal platform plays

4. **Energy Efficiency Moat (Underrated):**
   - 50,000x improvement means those with best efficiency serve cheapest
   - Compounds over billions of inferences
   - Hard to replicate without hardware expertise

**Time Horizon:**

*Short-term (0-2 years):*
- User adoption continues accelerating (ChatGPT to 1B+ users)
- Cost deflation enables new use cases (what's impossible at $X becomes viable at $X/100)
- Enterprise adoption scales (moving from pilots to production)
- Immediate benefit: productivity gains in knowledge work (coding, writing, research)

*Medium-term (2-5 years):*
- Infrastructure investments mature (data centers come online)
- Vertical applications emerge (healthcare, education, legal, etc.)
- Multi-cloud world fully realized (no single dominant provider)
- Energy solutions deployed (nuclear, grid upgrades)
- Competitive shakeout in application layer (most AI startups fail/get acquired)

*Long-term (5-10 years):*
- Hardware innovation (post-Volta) fully manifests in capabilities
- Agents become reliable enough for autonomous work
- Economic reorganization around AI productivity (job displacement/creation)
- Potential: AGI or near-AGI systems IF scaling laws continue

**Why Time Is Your Friend:**

The presenter's key insight: **"Hardware innovation can take years to unfold."**

The Volta example (2017-2018 breakthrough → 2022-2023 public manifestation) shows that:
1. Early infrastructure investments take 5-7 years to show full ROI
2. Being positioned BEFORE mainstream adoption creates asymmetric returns
3. The gap between capability (what's technically possible) and adoption (what's economically deployed) shrinks over time as costs fall

For investors/builders: Time is your friend IF you're building infrastructure or platforms. Time is your enemy IF you're building applications without moats, because convergence will commoditize your advantage.

The critical insight: **"No one uses Volta anymore. It's just that this innovation was enough to change the unit economics for AI."** Current investments (Hopper, Blackwell GPUs) will manifest in 2027-2030 capabilities.

**Lock-In Mechanisms:**

1. **Developer Ecosystem Lock-In (Nvidia CUDA):**
   - 6x developer growth in Nvidia ecosystem
   - Switching costs: Rewriting code, retraining teams, debugging new platforms
   - Network effects: More developers → Better tools → More developers
   - Time investment: Years of learning CUDA framework creates mental lock-in

2. **Infrastructure Sunk Costs:**
   - $100B+ invested in data centers optimized for specific GPU architectures
   - 2-3 year buildout cycles mean locked into architectural choices
   - Energy partnerships (nuclear agreements, grid capacity) are 10-20 year commitments
   - Geographic sovereign AI requirements lock in regional infrastructure

3. **Application Integration Lock-In:**
   - Microsoft Office integration with Copilot creates workflow dependency
   - Google Workspace integration with Gemini similar
   - Enterprise deployments involve months of integration work
   - Once productivity gains realized, switching costs are organizational disruption

4. **Habit Formation Lock-In:**
   - ChatGPT users spending 20+ minutes daily
   - Retention approaching Google Search levels (the gold standard)
   - Cognitive offloading: Once you rely on AI for certain tasks, reverting feels like regression
   - Muscle memory in workflows: keyboard shortcuts, prompt patterns, favorite features

**Compounding Effect:**

The system improves with use through:

1. **Data Compounding:** More users → More queries → Better understanding of what works → Improved models → More users

2. **Cost Compounding:** Economies of scale in data centers + algorithmic efficiency improvements + hardware iteration = exponential cost decline (99.7% in 2 years)

3. **Capability Compounding:** As models converge in performance, differentiation shifts to integration depth. Early integrators (Microsoft in enterprise, Google in consumer) compound advantage through years of workflow optimization.

4. **Infrastructure Compounding:** Each data center makes the next one easier (learnings on cooling, energy, networking). Each GPU generation builds on previous (Volta → Turing → Ampere → Hopper → Blackwell).

**The Critical Non-Linearity:**

The presenter identifies that **the 2019 inflection point** (visible in capex, developer growth, ML patents) took 3-4 years to manifest as ChatGPT. This lag is the flywheel's "hidden power"—it's spinning faster than anyone can see until suddenly it's obvious to everyone. By then, infrastructure advantages are locked in.

## 9. System Health Metric

**What to Optimize For:**

**Inference Cost per Useful Output (ICPUO)**

The ONE metric that matters most: **How much does it cost to generate a unit of value for an end user?**

This is better than:
- Raw inference cost (doesn't account for quality)
- Model performance benchmarks (don't account for cost)
- User growth (doesn't account for economics)
- Revenue (doesn't account for serving costs)

**Why This Metric:**

The presenter reveals the critical insight: **"Training costs exploding while inference costs collapse"** creates the entire business model tension in AI.

1. **It captures the unit economics paradox:**
   - Training costs up 2,400x (now $100B+ for frontier models)
   - Inference costs down 99.7% in 2 years
   - The gap between these determines viability

2. **It reveals sustainability:**
   - ChatGPT has massive user growth BUT inference costs scaling faster than revenue
   - The presenter notes: "There is a disconnect and they're going to have to figure out how to close that"
   - If ICPUO keeps falling, margins eventually appear
   - If it stalls, businesses are unsustainable

3. **It drives all strategic decisions:**
   - Whether to keep scaling model size (training costs)
   - Which models to deploy (inference costs)
   - What price to charge users (value capture)
   - Whether to invest in efficiency vs. capability

4. **It separates winners from losers:**
   - Those with best ICPUO can serve more users profitably
   - Those with worse ICPUO either raise prices (lose users) or burn cash
   - Over time, efficiency compounds and winners pull away

**The specific data point:** Going from 1.3 billion tokens per megawatt-year (early systems) to 65 trillion tokens per megawatt-year (current systems) represents a 50,000x improvement in energy efficiency. This is the kind of metric that matters—cost per unit of output.

**How to Measure:**

**For AI Infrastructure Companies:**

```
ICPUO = (Total Infrastructure Cost) / (Total Useful Tokens Generated)

Where:
- Infrastructure Cost includes: GPUs, data centers, energy, cooling, networking, maintenance
- Useful Tokens = Only tokens that reached users (not training, not failed inferences)
```

Track monthly. Goal: Down 50-100% year-over-year.

**For AI Application Companies:**

```
ICPUO = (API Costs + Overhead) / (User-Defined Value Units)

Where:
- API Costs = Inference charges from OpenAI/Anthropic/etc.
- Overhead = Engineering, product, support
- Value Units = Task completions, time saved, revenue enabled (context-dependent)
```

Track weekly. Goal: Down 20-50% quarter-over-quarter as you optimize prompts, switch models, cache effectively.

**For Enterprises Using AI:**

```
ICPUO = (AI Tool Subscriptions + Integration Costs + Training) / (Productivity Gains)

Where:
- Subscriptions = ChatGPT Plus, Copilot, etc.
- Integration Costs = IT time, workflow changes
- Training = Employee time learning tools
- Productivity Gains = Hours saved, tasks automated (measured via time studies)
```

Track quarterly. Goal: ROI positive within 6 months, 5x within 18 months.

**Leading Indicators to Watch:**

1. **Efficiency improvements from new GPU generations:** Each Nvidia release (Volta → Hopper → Blackwell) represents step-function improvement
2. **Algorithmic efficiency gains:** Same model, cheaper inference (model compression, quantization, distillation)
3. **Competition driving prices down:** When OpenAI/Anthropic/Google cut API prices, ICPUO improves for developers
4. **Energy costs:** Nuclear/grid improvements reduce denominator

**Lagging Indicators (What to Avoid):**

- Total capex (can be wasteful)
- Model parameter count (bigger ≠ better if costs explode)
- Benchmark scores (academic, not practical)
- Hype metrics (user signups without engagement)

**The Critical Insight:**

The presenter identifies that **"99.7% cost reduction in 2 years"** for inference is the real story. Compare to light bulb: 75 years for 99% cost reduction. AI is compressing decades of cost deflation into years.

IF this continues, every business model assumption changes. What's uneconomical today is viable tomorrow. This is why infrastructure bets make sense even without clear applications—the cost curve guarantees new use cases emerge.

## Strategic Patterns Identified

### Pattern 1: Hardware Innovation → Infrastructure Buildout → Application Explosion (5-10 Year Cycle)

**The Pattern:**
Major technological revolutions follow a predictable sequence with 5-10 year lag between stages. Hardware breakthrough (Nvidia Volta 2017) creates new unit economics → Infrastructure investments (2019 ML/data center capex) → Public awareness/application explosion (2022 ChatGPT) → Mass adoption (2023-present).

**Why It Matters:**
Understanding this lag allows strategic positioning in the "infrastructure buildout" phase before applications are obvious. The highest returns accrue to those who invest when smart money is building infrastructure but mainstream hasn't noticed. By the time applications are obvious, infrastructure advantages are locked in.

**How to Spot It:**
- Watch for developer ecosystem growth BEFORE consumer application growth (6x Nvidia developers pre-ChatGPT)
- Track capex/R&D inflection points BEFORE revenue justification
- Monitor GPU/chip roadmaps for step-function improvements (not incremental)
- Look for "boring" infrastructure investments that don't generate headlines

**Historical Examples:**
- Internet: TCP/IP (1970s) → ISP buildout (1990s) → Web boom (late 1990s) → Mass adoption (2000s)
- Mobile: 3G infrastructure (2000s) → App Store (2008) → Mobile-first companies (2010s)
- Cloud: Data center buildout (2006-2010) → SaaS explosion (2010-2015)

### Pattern 2: Cost Deflation Drives Adoption (Exponential Timeline Compression)

**The Pattern:**
Technology adoption accelerates when costs fall 99%+ in compressed timeframes. Historical technology cost reductions took decades (light bulb: 75 years). Modern digital technologies compress this to years (ChatGPT: 2 years for 99.7% inference cost reduction). Each generation of technology demonstrates faster cost deflation than the previous generation.

**Why It Matters:**
What's economically impossible today becomes trivial tomorrow. Strategic planning must account for exponential cost deflation, not linear. Business models should assume 50-100% annual inference cost reductions for next 3-5 years. This creates opportunities for "land grab" strategies—capture market share while costs are falling faster than prices.

**How to Spot It:**
- API pricing changes happening quarterly (not annually)
- New GPU generations showing 5-10x efficiency gains
- Algorithmic improvements (model compression, quantization) published monthly
- Competitive pressure driving prices down even as capability improves

**The Trap:**
Building business models based on current costs. If your unit economics assume today's inference costs, and costs fall 90% in 18 months, competitors will undercut you. Better: Build business model assuming costs approach zero, capture value through integration/brand/data.

### Pattern 3: Proliferation Over Concentration (Multi-Cloud Reality)

**The Pattern:**
Unlike previous technology eras with clear winners (Microsoft/Windows, Google/Search, AWS/Cloud initially), AI is proliferating across multiple providers due to: (1) Technique sharing accelerates model convergence, (2) Sovereign/regulatory requirements force geographic distribution, (3) Switching costs are lower (API compatibility), (4) Training is expensive but inference is cheap (democratized access).

**Why It Matters:**
Strategies based on "picking the winner" or "platform lock-in" will fail. Better: Assume multi-cloud world where you need flexibility to switch providers as costs/capabilities shift. Build integrations that work across providers. Competitive advantage comes from HOW you use AI (workflows, data, integration depth) not WHICH AI you use.

**How to Spot It:**
- Major players (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta) all viable simultaneously
- Open source models (Llama) closing gap with proprietary models
- Enterprises deploying multi-model strategies (GPT for X, Claude for Y, Gemini for Z)
- API standardization efforts (OpenAI-compatible endpoints)

**Strategic Implication:**
For 1658 Holdings companies: Don't over-invest in single-vendor relationship. Build data pipelines and workflows that can leverage multiple AI providers. Competitive advantage is proprietary data + integration depth, not exclusive model access.
