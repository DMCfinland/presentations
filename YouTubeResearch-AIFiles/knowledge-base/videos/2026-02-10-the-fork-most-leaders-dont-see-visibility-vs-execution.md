# The Fork Most Leaders Don't See: Visibility vs. Execution

## Summary

This video presents a critical strategic fork facing organizations in the AI era: whether to use AI for top-down visibility (the "magnifying glass company") or for bottom-up execution leverage (the "tiger team company"). The core insight is that AI's reduction in the cost of legibility creates a dangerous trap—leaders can now generate perfect-looking dashboards, metrics, and reports without understanding that this "fake legibility" obscures where real value is created. The winning strategy is to use AI to amplify small, trusted teams (tiger teams) that do illegible but essential work, while using AI only as a "cheap historian" to translate their messy reality into digestible reports after the fact, not to control work before it happens.

---

## 1. Context

**Background:** 

AI has made it dramatically cheaper for companies to see what's happening inside themselves—examining PRs, Slack threads, code diffs, meeting notes, and tickets. This has spawned a wave of "single pane of glass" enterprise visibility solutions. Simultaneously, AI is enabling small teams to accomplish what previously required 20-30 people. This creates a strategic choice: invest AI capacity in visibility systems or in execution leverage.

The video draws inspiration from Shan Gade's essay on "legible and illegible work." Legible work shows up in Jira, OKRs, and roadmaps—it's planned, trackable, and explainable. Illegible work is the harsh reality underneath: favors, back channels, shared intuition, quick fixes, tiger teams, and the work that happens when companies suspend formal processes during emergencies.

**Why This Matters:** 

This is strategically relevant because it addresses the fundamental question of where AI creates competitive advantage. Companies that optimize for visibility will create bureaucratic overhead and drive real work underground. Companies that optimize for execution leverage will move faster than traditional competitors while maintaining enough legibility to coordinate. The strategic choice determines whether AI amplifies or suffocates organizational velocity.

For 1658 Holdings, this framework offers a lens to evaluate: (1) how portfolio companies should deploy AI resources, (2) how to structure teams for maximum velocity, and (3) how to design reporting systems that don't kill innovation.

**Key Stats:**
- AI drops the cost of legibility-creation activities "down to zero"
- A 5-person AI-powered pod can now produce what took 20-30 people in traditional structures
- Companies like Amazon are firing middle management whose primary job was information aggregation
- The speaker references two engineers whose weekend projects (illegible work) created more value than planned OKR work

## 3. Strategic Engine

**How This Actually Works:**

The tiger team model works by reversing the traditional relationship between work and visibility. Instead of forcing work into legible formats before it happens (planning, tickets, OKRs that constrain action), the system allows small, empowered teams to execute with velocity, then uses AI to create legibility after the fact.

The mechanism:
1. Small teams (5-ish people, cross-functional) own clear outcomes
2. Teams have AI leverage for coding, debugging, synthesis, customer understanding
3. Teams operate with "fast lanes"—ability to spike on problems without permission
4. AI translates the messy artifact trail (code, conversations, docs) into legible reports
5. Leadership uses these reports to understand and coordinate, not to control

**Key Components:**

1. **Tiger Teams as Primary Production Units:** Small, sovereign teams with clear scope and outcomes, not departments or functions
2. **AI as Execution Leverage:** Teams use AI for faster coding, option exploration, debugging, synthesis—not for reporting
3. **Shared Context/Trust/Taste:** Teams operate with enough shared understanding to coordinate without formal handoffs
4. **Fast Lanes/Emergency Mode:** Protected paths for solving urgent problems without bureaucratic approval
5. **AI as "Cheap Historian":** Translation layer that reconstructs meaning from artifact trails after work happens

**Why This Works:**

The underlying logic is that **the cost structure of coordination has fundamentally changed**. Previously, the friction of coordination meant you needed formal processes to align 20-30 people. But if 5 people can do what 20 did, and if AI can translate their messy work into legible summaries cheaply, you can optimize for velocity rather than control.

The system works because it acknowledges reality: "When something really important happens in your work, who do people go to? When the database hits a limit, who do people go to? Right? When a top customer is on fire, who do people go to? The company will tell the truth when there's an emergency."

## 4. Behavioral Design

**Behavioral Principles:**

1. **Sovereignty enables ownership:** Teams with clear scope and authority take responsibility for outcomes
2. **Messiness signals innovation:** The best work often looks disorganized because it's exploratory
3. **Trust reduces coordination tax:** Shared context means less need for formal handoffs
4. **Speed creates momentum:** Fast lanes and spike mode generate compounding velocity
5. **Legibility follows work, doesn't precede it:** Reporting is translation, not control

**Incentive Structure:**

**The system encourages:**
- Taking initiative on problems within team scope
- Weekend/exploratory work on mission-aligned problems
- Cross-functional collaboration without permission
- Using AI for execution leverage (coding, analysis, synthesis)
- Delivering outcomes over documenting process

**The system discourages:**
- Optimizing for metrics instead of outcomes
- Hiding real work to meet formal expectations
- Waiting for approval before solving obvious problems
- Using AI for surveillance or micro-tracking
- Generating "vapor metrics" (AI-generated scores without concrete actions)

**Alignment Mechanisms:**

1. **Clear Outcomes:** Teams aligned on what success looks like, not how to achieve it
2. **Shared Mission:** Cross-functional teams organized around customer/business objectives, not job families
3. **After-the-Fact Translation:** AI creates legibility for coordination without controlling work
4. **Emergency Mode Recognition:** Formal acknowledgment of fast paths for critical work
5. **Leadership Restraint:** Leaders resist the seductive "omniscience" feeling AI visibility creates

## 5. Time & Attention

**Where Time Flows:**

In the tiger team model:
- **70% Execution:** Coding, building, testing, customer conversations, problem-solving
- **20% Coordination:** Within-team sync, cross-team handoffs based on shared context
- **10% Translation:** Creating legibility for external stakeholders using AI

In the magnifying glass model (the trap):
- **30% Execution:** Actual building (constrained by oversight)
- **40% Documentation:** Feeding visibility systems (tickets, status updates, metric dashboards)
- **30% Coordination:** Managing handoffs through formal processes

**What This System DOESN'T Spend On:**

- **Status meetings:** AI can answer "what changed last week" from artifacts
- **Ticket ceremony:** No need to write perfect tickets before doing exploratory work
- **Management layers:** Direct visibility into artifact trails eliminates need for human aggregators
- **Perfect planning:** Teams can spike and explore, then rationalize afterward
- **Bureaucratic approval:** Fast lanes eliminate waiting for permission on urgent work
- **Fake metrics:** No time wasted generating dashboards that don't drive decisions

**Allocation Philosophy:**

"Let that legibility, let the AI reporting follow behind the work. Don't let it dictate it."

The core principle is that **time is a team's scarcest resource in the AI era**. If AI can make 5 people as productive as 20, the bottleneck shifts from labor to attention. Spending attention on legibility theater (making work visible before it happens) destroys the velocity advantage AI creates. The winning move is to spend attention on execution leverage and use AI's cheap synthesis capabilities for coordination after the fact.

## 9. System Health Metric

**What to Optimize For:**

**Outcome Velocity Per Team**—measured as meaningful customer/business outcomes delivered per team per quarter, not tickets closed or lines of code.

Specifically: Track whether small teams are delivering complete solutions (like the accessibility feature or ML artwork solution in the speaker's story) that weren't in the formal roadmap but created real value.

**Why This Metric:**

This metric captures the essence of the tiger team model:
1. **Outcomes not outputs:** Measures what matters, not what's easy to track
2. **Per-team normalization:** Accounts for team size without rewarding bloat
3. **Velocity component:** Values speed of delivery, not just volume
4. **Illegible work inclusion:** Captures weekend projects and off-roadmap work that formal metrics miss

As the speaker emphasizes: "Please, this means you should measure your teams by their outcomes, by the impact that they deliver, not by just adherence to an AI generated plan."

**How to Measure:**

**Practical implementation:**

1. **Define "meaningful outcome":** Customer problem solved, business metric moved, technical debt eliminated, new capability enabled
2. **Quarterly team retrospectives:** Each team identifies 3-5 outcomes they delivered (planned and unplanned)
3. **AI-assisted synthesis:** Use LLMs to analyze artifact trails and validate claimed outcomes against code changes, customer feedback, metrics
4. **Leadership validation:** Review outcomes for genuine impact vs. activity theater
5. **Track trend over time:** Are teams delivering more outcomes per quarter? Are unplanned outcomes increasing (sign of healthy illegible work)?

**Anti-metrics to avoid:**
- Story points completed
- Lines of code written
- Tickets closed
- Meeting attendance
- Documentation completeness
- Any metric that can be gamed without delivering value

**Diagnostic questions:**
- Are teams delivering outcomes that weren't in the formal roadmap?
- Are engineers bringing in weekend projects that create value?
- Do customers notice improvements without prompting?
- Can teams spike on urgent problems without waiting for approval?

### Non-Obvious Insights

- **The cost collapse creates a trap, not just an opportunity:** When AI makes legibility cheap, it also makes fake legibility (vapor metrics, meaningless dashboards) even cheaper. The ease of generation doesn't correlate with value.

- **Legibility has always had a natural cost constraint:** Before AI, the human effort required to make work visible limited how much visibility companies could demand. AI removes this constraint, enabling unlimited visibility theater.

- **Cross-functional tiger teams dissolve job family distinctions:** The speaker envisions teams with "sales and CS and legal and finance and ops all on the same mission using AI to understand each other's work"—this is radically different from functional hierarchies.

- **AI as historian vs. bureaucrat:** The key distinction isn't whether to use AI for legibility, but when—after work (translation) vs. before work (control). The timing determines whether AI amplifies or suffocates.

- **Weekend work is a feature, not a problem:** The engineer who hacks together solutions on weekends because they care about the problem is exactly what companies should nurture, not eliminate through time tracking.

- **Trust is the real bottleneck:** In the speaker's story, the key moment wasn't the weekend project—it was the product leader saying "I don't care if it's in the OKRs. Let's wrap it in and get it moving." Trust enables velocity.

## 11. Application & Mental Model

### When to Use This Pattern

**Signals indicating tiger team model is appropriate:**

1. **Ambiguous problem spaces:** When solutions aren't obvious and require exploration
2. **Fast-moving markets:** When competitor velocity matters more than perfect coordination
3. **Talent-constrained environments:** When great people are scarce and must be leveraged maximally
4. **High trust cultures:** When teams have demonstrated ability to self-organize effectively
5. **AI-native work:** When AI tools can genuinely amplify small team productivity (software, analysis, content)
6. **Customer-driven work:** When proximity to customer problems matters more than internal process
7. **Innovation required:** When breakthrough solutions come from exploration, not optimization

**Organizational conditions:**
- Leadership willing to tolerate messiness and partial visibility
- Clear outcome definition (even if methods are flexible)
- Cross-functional skills exist or can be developed
- Compensation/promotion not tied to headcount management
- Minimal regulatory/compliance constraints that require perfect audit trails

### When NOT to Use This Pattern

**Signals indicating this pattern will backfire:**

1. **High-compliance environments:** Regulatory requirements may demand perfect legibility (healthcare, finance)
2. **Low-trust cultures:** If teams haven't earned autonomy, granting it creates chaos
3. **Commodity/process work:** When execution is well-understood and scaling matters more than innovation
4. **Very large scale coordination:** Some problems genuinely require 100+ person coordination (enterprise sales, massive infrastructure)
5. **Zero-defect requirements:** When mistakes are catastrophic (safety-critical systems)
6. **Political/visibility-driven leadership:** If leadership derives power from control, they'll sabotage tiger teams
7. **Weak shared context:** If teams don't have common language, frameworks, or goals

**When the magnifying glass model might be legitimate:**
- Due diligence for investors/auditors (but use AI as historian, not controller)
- Crisis diagnosis when something is genuinely broken
- Regulatory compliance and audit trails
- Large-scale resource allocation decisions
- Identifying structural bottlenecks or misalignments

**Red flags suggesting pattern isn't working:**
- Tiger teams becoming cliques that exclude others
- Weekend work becoming expectation rather than passion project
- Lack of legibility creating coordination failures
- Outcomes declining despite high activity
- Talent leaving because of burnout or lack of structure

**Finland DMC Oy:**

1. **Immediate Application—AI-Leveraged Service Design:**
   - Form cross-functional pods (sales + operations + product) around specific customer segments or service types
   - Use AI for faster customer research synthesis, service design exploration, operational optimization
   - Let teams spike on customer problems without waiting for formal approval
   - Use AI to translate messy exploration into client-ready proposals after the fact

   - Reorganize from functional silos to outcome-based pods (e.g., "corporate events team," "luxury travel team")
   - Give pods autonomy over pricing, service design, vendor relationships within clear parameters
   - Use AI visibility tools only for cross-pod coordination and client reporting, not oversight
   - Expected outcome: Reduced handoff friction, faster decision-making, better outcomes per team

3. **Cultural Shift—Protect Illegible Work:**
   - Celebrate examples of employees proactively solving customer problems outside formal process
   - Create explicit "fast lanes" for urgent customer issues
   - Reduce status meeting overhead by using AI to synthesize activity from emails, Slack, CRM
   - Expected outcome: Higher employee engagement, more innovation, better client satisfaction

**General Principles for All Portfolio Companies:**

1. **Use AI for execution leverage first, visibility second:**
   - Invest in AI tools that help teams code faster, analyze better, synthesize information more effectively
   - Only then invest in AI tools that create visibility for coordination
   - Never use AI visibility tools for micromanagement or surveillance
   - Rule of thumb: If the tool helps teams ship faster, invest; if it helps managers feel better, scrutinize

2. **Structure teams around outcomes, not functions:**
   - Identify 3-5 most important business outcomes (customer acquisition, retention, product innovation, etc.)
   - Form cross-functional pods around each outcome
   - Give pods clear metrics but autonomy over methods
   - Use AI to translate pod activity into legible reports for stakeholders

3. **Create fast lanes and protect messiness:**
   - Establish explicit "spike mode" or "tiger team" protocols for urgent work
   - Celebrate weekend projects and off-roadmap innovation that aligns with mission
   - Reduce ceremony (status meetings, ticket bureaucracy) by using AI for synthesis
   - Judge outcomes, not process adherence

4. **Be skeptical of AI visibility vendors:**
   - If vendor pitches "single pane of glass" or "see everything," ask: "Does this help teams execute or help managers feel omniscient?"
   - If vendor can't articulate how tool improves outcomes (not just visibility), pass
   - Prefer tools that translate messy reality after the fact to tools that control before

5. **Track outcome velocity per team as North Star:**
   - Measure meaningful outcomes delivered per quarter per team
   - Include unplanned/off-roadmap work in accounting
   - Use AI to validate outcomes against artifacts, but don't let measurement become the goal
   - If outcome velocity declines, diagnose whether it's due to real problems or visibility theater

6. **Resist the omniscience temptation:**
   - When AI makes it easy to see everything, consciously choose not to
   - Ask: "Does knowing this help coordinate or does it enable micromanagement?"
   - Trust tiger teams until they demonstrate they shouldn't be trusted
   - Remember the speaker's warning: "Don't mistake that [AI visibility] for control or even necessarily for clarity"

## Strategic Patterns Identified

1. **The Execution vs. Oversight Trap:** When new technology drops the cost of visibility, organizations often over-invest in oversight at the expense of execution. The winner is whoever resists this temptation and invests in leverage instead.

2. **Small Team Leverage Pattern:** When AI amplifies individual productivity, the optimal organizational structure shifts from large coordinated teams to small autonomous teams with just-enough coordination. This is the "power pack for small teams" insight.

3. **Messy Reality Preservation Pattern:** The best organizations protect spaces where work can be illegible, messy, and exploratory—because that's where innovation happens. Systems that demand perfect legibility drive real work underground and create "fake legibility" theater.
