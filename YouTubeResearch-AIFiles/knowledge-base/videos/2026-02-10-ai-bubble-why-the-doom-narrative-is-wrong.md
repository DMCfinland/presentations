# AI Bubble? Why the Doom Narrative is Wrong

## Summary
This video provides a counternarrative to the "AI bubble" doom narrative by analyzing five overlooked facts: chatbot saturation is real but expected, progress is moving to complex agentic use cases, exponential gains continue on non-saturated benchmarks, chip underallocation signals massive demand, and teams are strategically refocusing. The core insight is that we're in a power-law game where rational actors are making heavy, concentrated bets in specific niches—behavior that looks frothy but is actually strategic given the exponential returns. The real story isn't bubble dynamics but rather the difficult transition from simple consumer use cases to complex business tools that deliver 10x gains for the 5% who get it right.

## 1. Context

**Background:** In early 2025, several events converged to create a "death of AI" narrative: GPT-5's disappointing rollout, Meta AI division layoffs/restructuring, Sam Altman's admission of bubble elements, and an MIT study showing most enterprise AI projects fail. These created a pendulum swing from hype to doom.

**Why This Matters:** This represents a critical inflection point in AI adoption where surface-level narratives diverge sharply from underlying fundamentals. Understanding this gap is essential for strategic capital allocation—both companies overinvesting in wrong areas and underinvesting in right areas are making costly mistakes. The video provides a framework for distinguishing signal from noise in AI investment decisions.

**Key Stats:** 
- 95% of organizations failing at AI (MIT study)
- 100% of organizations desperately trying to get into AI (implied)
- MER benchmark showing exponential gains, doubling every few months
- Power law dynamics across multiple dimensions (talent, chips, returns)

## 2. Vision & Why

**Core Mission:** To provide a more accurate, nuanced understanding of AI progress that moves beyond simple bubble/not-bubble framing to recognize the power-law dynamics at play.

**The "Why" Behind It:** People need better mental models for understanding technology adoption curves, especially when dealing with exponential technologies that don't follow linear intuition. The chatbot use case set false expectations—it was unusually accessible and visceral, making the shift to complex agentic systems feel like failure rather than evolution.

**Enduring Nature:** 
- **Timeless:** Power law dynamics in technology adoption, the difference between innovation types (human intuition vs. brute force computation), the lag between technological capability and business value capture
- **2024-2026 Specific:** The particular transition from GPT-4 level chatbots to agentic systems, the specific players (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, XAI), the chip shortage dynamics, the MIT study timing

## 3. Strategic Engine

**How This Actually Works:** AI is demonstrating real value in narrow use cases, creating power-law returns that justify disproportionate investment by those who identify their correct niche. Meanwhile, the 95% failure rate signals enormous demand—these failures represent companies betting so much they'll keep trying until they succeed. The combination of exponential capability improvement + constrained chip supply + demonstrated 10x gains for winners creates rational conditions for what looks like frothy investment.

**Key Components:**
1. **Exponential capability growth** in AI models on non-saturated benchmarks (MER, complex reasoning)
2. **Power law return structure** that rewards heavy, concentrated bets in correct niches
3. **Niche specialization pressure** forcing players to pick specific value chains (cloud, models, applications)
4. **High failure rate signaling high demand** (95% failing because 100% are trying)
5. **Supply constraints** (chips) indicating genuine scarcity economics, not bubble excess

**Why This Works:** The system self-corrects through brutal selection pressure. Players who don't find their niche or who spread resources too thin get winnowed out (Amazon AI, potentially Apple). Those who concentrate capital in defendable niches where they can capture power law returns (Microsoft/cloud, OpenAI/frontier models, Anthropic/coding) compound advantages over time.

## 4. Behavioral Design

**Behavioral Principles:**
- **Narrative hunger:** People need story swings to process complex information—from GPT-5 hype to bubble doom to (eventually) realistic assessment
- **Perception vs. reality gaps:** Chatbot progress feels saturated because user perception plateaus even as underlying capability improves
- **Visibility bias:** Simple, consumer-facing use cases (chatbots) are overcovered while complex business tools generating 10x gains remain invisible
- **Power law blindness:** Linear intuition fails to grasp exponential growth, making rapid progress look suspicious

**Incentive Structure:**
- **Encourages:** Niche identification, heavy capital allocation in chosen domains, strategic patience
- **Discourages:** Spray-and-pray approaches, me-too product copying, premature dismissal based on consumer metrics
- **Punishes:** Late movers who didn't build capability early, generalists who refuse to specialize

**Alignment Mechanisms:** Market pressure forces specialization—companies must choose between being model makers, infrastructure providers, or application builders. Trying to do multiple creates capital inefficiency that power law dynamics punish harshly.

## 9. System Health Metric

**What to Optimize For:** **Niche-Specific ROI × Capital Efficiency Ratio**

More specifically: (Value captured in chosen niche) / (Capital deployed across all AI initiatives)

**Why This Metric:** This captures the essential insight that success in AI isn't about total investment or general "being in AI"—it's about concentrated, effective deployment in a defensible niche. A company spending $1M in the right place can outperform one spending $100M across wrong areas. This metric forces clarity on:
1. Have we identified our actual niche? (numerator specificity)
2. Are we concentrating resources there? (denominator discipline)
3. Are we capturing power law returns? (ratio magnitude)

**How to Measure:**
1. **Define niche clearly:** Not "AI strategy" but "AI-powered customer service in Nordic travel" or "AI code generation for internal tools"
2. **Measure actual business impact:** Revenue increase, cost reduction, time savings—quantifiable 10x-type gains
3. **Audit full AI spending:** Include experiments, pilots, me-too features, hype-driven initiatives
4. **Calculate ratio monthly:** Track whether concentration is increasing (fewer, bigger bets) and whether returns per dollar are improving
5. **Benchmark against alternatives:** Is this better than next-best use of capital?

### Non-Obvious Insights

- **Chatbot saturation is a feature, not a bug:** The fact that chatbots feel "done" is evidence of success reaching a natural ceiling, not AI progress stopping. It reveals the difference between accessible-but-limited use cases and complex-but-valuable ones.

- **High failure rates signal high demand, not low value:** The 95% enterprise AI failure rate is being misread—it actually demonstrates that AI value is so compelling that organizations will keep trying despite repeated failure. This is opposite of bubble dynamics where people chase purely speculative value.

- **Chip underallocation proves we're not in a bubble:** Real bubbles are characterized by overcapacity and oversupply. The fact that frontier labs can't get enough chips to serve demand indicates genuine scarcity economics, not speculative excess.

- **The visibility problem masks real progress:** Mathematical proofs and complex agentic workflows don't make good demos, so progress feels slower than it is. The most valuable AI work is increasingly invisible to non-experts, creating perception lag.

- **Power law dynamics force ruthless specialization:** The winnowing from many AI players to a handful isn't market failure—it's the natural result of power law economics where concentrated bets in correct niches dramatically outperform diversified approaches.

- **Narrative swings are a feature of information processing:** People's need to swing from hype to doom isn't irrational—it's how collective intelligence processes complex, fast-moving information. The problem is mistaking the processing mechanism for the underlying reality.

- **Me-too behavior proves value exists:** The rush to copy Lovable's vibe coding approach (including by AirTable) demonstrates validated demand. In actual bubbles, there's no clear value to copy—just speculation.

- **Restructuring is refocusing, not retreat:** Meta's AI division restructuring is being misread as contraction when it's actually strategic reallocation toward inference and away from saturated areas—exactly what you'd do if you understood the power law game.

- **Brute force innovation is different from creative innovation:** AI models innovate by exhaustively exploring defined problem spaces (mathematical proofs) rather than intuitive leaps. This is valuable but different, and the difference matters for understanding where AI adds value.

- **The 5% success stories aren't being told:** The narrative focuses on aggregate failure rates while the transformative successes remain largely invisible. This creates systematic pessimism bias that misallocates capital away from real opportunities.

## 11. Application & Mental Model

### When to Use This Pattern

**Signal conditions:**
- Technology showing exponential capability growth on objective benchmarks
- High variance in outcomes (some winners getting 10x+ returns, many getting zero)
- Supply constraints on critical inputs (chips, talent, data) despite high investment
- Narrative volatility swinging between hype and doom
- Visible me-too behavior copying validated approaches
- Major players making large, concentrated bets in specific niches
- High failure rates accompanied by persistent retry behavior

**This pattern applies when:**
- You're trying to distinguish genuine power law opportunities from bubbles
- You need to decide between diversified "being in the space" vs. concentrated niche bets
- You're evaluating whether apparent market froth is rational or speculative
- You're setting AI strategy and need to resist both hype and doom narratives

### When NOT to Use This Pattern

**This pattern fails when:**
- Technology progress is actually plateauing (not just appearing to due to measurement challenges)
- High investment is driven by FOMO rather than demonstrated returns
- Supply is expanding faster than demand (opposite of chip situation)
- Me-too behavior has no validated value underneath it
- "Power law returns" are claimed but not demonstrated with concrete examples
- Narrative consensus is uniformly bullish (actual bubble condition)

**Warning signs to watch:**
- If chip supply suddenly exceeds demand, power law logic breaks
- If the 95% failure rate stops leading to retry behavior, demand isn't real
- If model capability improvements slow on ALL benchmarks (not just saturated ones)
- If niche winners can't articulate concrete moats or show actual 10x gains

## Strategic Patterns Identified

1. **Power Law Capital Allocation Pattern:** In markets with exponential capability growth and supply constraints, concentrated bets in defensible niches dramatically outperform diversified approaches. This creates appearance of froth (big bets) that is actually rational given return structure. Key signal: Are winners demonstrating 10x+ gains while supply (chips, talent) remains constrained?

2. **Exponential Growth Masking Pattern:** When technology progresses exponentially but user-visible improvements saturate (chatbots), narrative swings to "progress stopped" while underlying capability growth continues. This creates systematic mispricing of ongoing opportunity. Key insight: Distinguish between interface saturation and capability plateaus.

3. **Demand Signal Inversion Pattern:** High failure rates in nascent technology adoption signal high demand (organizations trying despite difficulty) rather than low value. This inverts normal interpretation where high failure = low opportunity. Works because demonstrated successes (10x gains) create existential competitive pressure that sustains retry behavior despite failures.
