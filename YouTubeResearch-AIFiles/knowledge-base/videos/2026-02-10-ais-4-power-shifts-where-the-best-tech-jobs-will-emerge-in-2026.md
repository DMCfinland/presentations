# AI's 4 Power Shifts: Where the Best Tech Jobs Will Emerge in 2026

## Summary
Nate B Jones identifies four fundamental dynamics reshaping tech employment: (1) execution getting cheaper through AI automation, (2) speed creating quality/security nightmares that generate new jobs, (3) compute costs exploding and creating infrastructure roles, and (4) the human-AI boundary crisis requiring new interaction design. Rather than AI eliminating jobs, these dynamics create a complex landscape where roles evolve around their "beating heart"—the core accountability that AI cannot replace. The strategic insight: jobs grow where AI creates problems, not where it provides solutions. Companies should focus hiring on accountability roles (PM, program management, directors), human-facing roles (customer success, UX), and AI-infrastructure roles (MLOps, data engineering, security) while recognizing that middle management and pure execution roles face compression.

## 3. Strategic Engine

**How This Actually Works:**
The job market operates on a paradox: AI simultaneously destroys and creates jobs, but not in the ways commonly assumed. The engine works through four dynamics that create opposing forces:

1. **Execution cheapening** (job destruction pressure)
2. **Quality/security crises** (job creation from #1's problems)
3. **Infrastructure explosion** (job creation from scale)
4. **Human-AI boundary crisis** (job creation from confusion)

These dynamics don't cancel out—they create a new equilibrium where certain roles compress (middle management, pure execution) while others expand (accountability, human-facing, infrastructure).

**Key Components:**

1. **The "Beating Heart" Analysis**: Every role has a core that AI cannot replace—the fundamental accountability or human skill. Product managers' beating heart is "earning trust in chaos," not writing docs. Engineers' beating heart is "designing durable technical systems," not writing code.

2. **Problem-Following Strategy**: Jobs emerge where AI creates problems. Vibe coding creates security review jobs. Fast execution creates quality assurance jobs. GPU fleets create optimization jobs.

3. **Cost Dynamics**: When execution becomes cheap (via AI), craft becomes valuable. When infrastructure becomes expensive (GPU costs), optimization becomes valuable. The ratio determines job demand.

4. **Accountability Premium**: As systems become more automated and complex, clear points of human accountability become more valuable, not less. Directors and program managers survive by holding accountability that cannot be delegated to AI.

5. **Human-AI Interface Layer**: An entire job category emerges around managing the boundary between humans and AI—UX designers, psychologists on red teams, business process designers, interaction specialists.

**Why This Works:**
The framework succeeds because it:
- Identifies **what AI cannot do** (accountability, trust-building, relationship management) rather than focusing on what it can do
- Recognizes that **speed creates problems** as much as solutions
- Understands that **scale drives specialization** (trillion-dollar infrastructure requires specialists)
- Sees that **confusion is expensive** (human-AI boundary issues cost money, creating jobs)

## 4. Behavioral Design

**Behavioral Principles:**

1. **Humans correct better than they check**: "Humans are better at correcting mistakes we have made than checking our work. Which is why good email systems will often give you a delay on send and an undo button." This principle should drive all AI interface design.

2. **Accountability cannot be distributed to AI**: People instinctively want a human to blame/praise. Even as AI does more work, human accountability points become more valuable.

3. **Trust earned through chaos management**: In AI-accelerated environments with more chaos, the ability to create order and trust is the premium skill.

4. **Relationship depth over transaction speed**: As transactions become automated, relationship-holding becomes the differentiator for roles like customer success.

**Incentive Structure:**
The system **encourages**:
- Learning AI tools to amplify output (survival level)
- Developing complementary skills to AI (adaptation level)
- Building frameworks others can use (leadership level)
- Taking clear accountability (premium compensation)

The system **discourages**:
- Pure execution roles without judgment
- Information filtering without value-add (middle management)
- Hiding behind AI outputs without verification
- Avoiding technical depth

**Alignment Mechanisms:**
- **Market pressure**: Companies paying premium for roles with clear accountability
- **Quality crises**: Vibe-coded disasters creating demand for cleanup
- **Regulatory pressure**: Compliance requirements creating structured roles
- **Cost pressure**: Infrastructure costs creating optimization roles

**Winners:**

2. **Human-facing specialists (Customer Success, UX Designers, Sales Engineers)**
   - **How they win**: "Their success does not depend on their ability to answer tickets. Their success depends on their ability to hold relationships. That is a human thing." As transactions automate, relationship depth becomes the moat.

3. **Infrastructure specialists (Cloud AI Engineers, MLOps, Vector Database Engineers)**
   - **How they win**: "An engineer that can do his or her job well pays for their salary 10 or 100 times over in the way they handle these larger and larger fleets of GPUs." Scale creates specialist demand.

4. **Quality/Security specialists (AI Security, Red Team, QA transitioning to continuous monitoring)**
   - **How they win**: Speed creates quality crises. "There are new security and quality challenges with vibe coding... that's where I see really compelling PM action and nobody is talking about it."

5. **Data specialists (Data Scientists, Data Engineers)**
   - **How they win**: "Most of the failures I see in AI projects come from the data side." Preparing data for AI is custom work that can't be easily automated.

6. **Core Engineers (Software Engineers with fundamentals)**
   - **How they win**: "You must learn how technical systems get put together because that is actually the path toward career leadership." Fundamentals become more valuable as AI handles routine coding.

**Losers (or roles under severe pressure):**

1. **Middle Managers / Senior Managers / Directors (span compression)**
   - **Why**: "Middle managers are fundamentally information bottlenecks. Their entire job for most of the history of corporations has been to filter information. Well, guess what? LLMs are already really good at filtering information."

   - **Why**: "If you need to learn hard skills in AI, the only way to learn it is by building an AI now yourself or by learning it on the job. Academics isn't keeping up."

   - **Why**: "Most QA people that I talk to are not ready for this world. They are used to P0, P1, P2, do the test and launch. That mindset won't work."
   - **Shift needed**: From pre-launch testing to continuous production quality monitoring.

4. **MBA-track PMs without technical depth**
   - **Why**: "MBAs aren't learning this stuff. If you need to learn hard skills in AI, the only way to learn it is by building."
   - **The gap**: Business school doesn't teach AI fundamentals fast enough.

## 9. System Health Metric

**What to Optimize For:**
**The Accountability-to-Automation Ratio**: As a company deploys more AI automation, is it proportionally strengthening human accountability points, or is it creating a "hollow middle" where work happens but no one is responsible?

**Why This Metric:**
This captures the core dynamic: AI can speed execution, but organizations need stronger (not weaker) accountability as complexity and speed increase. Companies optimizing this ratio:
- Deploy AI aggressively (high automation)
- Simultaneously strengthen accountability roles (PM, directors, program managers)
- Create clear ownership of AI outputs
- Avoid the "AI did it" responsibility diffusion

The ratio prevents two failure modes:
1. **Too much automation, too little accountability**: Fast but chaotic, quality disasters, no one responsible
2. **Too little automation, too much oversight**: Slow, expensive, missing AI productivity gains

**How to Measure:**

**Quantitative proxies:**
1. **Accountability clarity score**: Survey employees: "Do you know who is accountable for X decision?" Track trend as AI deployment increases.

2. **Quality incident ownership time**: Time from incident to clear human owner identified. Should decrease even as AI usage increases.

3. **AI output verification rate**: % of AI-generated work reviewed by accountable humans before deployment. Should be high for production, lower for exploration.

4. **Span of accountability**: Average number of direct reports for accountability roles (PM, directors). Should increase moderately with AI tools, but not so much that accountability diffuses.

**Qualitative assessment:**
- Can you name the human accountable for each major AI-driven initiative?
- Do AI tools amplify accountable humans, or replace them?
- When AI creates a problem, is there a clear escalation path to a human decision-maker?

**For 1658 Holdings specifically:**
Track this ratio as Finland DMC Oy adopts AI tools:
- **Automation axis**: % of customer queries handled by AI, % of itinerary generation AI-assisted, % of operations planning AI-supported
- **Accountability axis**: Clarity of ownership for customer experience, quality control processes, escalation paths

Target: Automation should grow faster than team size, but accountability clarity should remain constant or improve.

### Non-Obvious Insights

- **Chaos creates trust-building opportunities**: Most see AI as reducing the need for human coordination. Nate argues AI's chaos-creation actually increases the value of people who can build trust and order. The PM role becomes more valuable precisely because AI makes things more chaotic.

- **Quality work becomes more valuable as cheap work proliferates**: "As execution gets faster, UI craft is becoming more valuable because the cheap stuff is becoming more commoditized. So if you have really really polished UI, it's going to stand out more because the sea of the internet is going to be a bunch of vibecoded stuff that is not well-crafted."

- **The "beating heart" is what AI cannot do, not what AI augments**: Most analysis focuses on "how AI helps X role." Nate flips this: identify what AI fundamentally cannot do (accountability, relationships, trust) and that's where the role's value concentrates.

- **Middle management compression is not about AI capability but information flow**: It's not that AI can "manage people." It's that AI eliminates the information-filtering function that justified middle management layers. The remaining directors will have much larger spans because they're not bottlenecks anymore.

- **Speed creates its own job category**: "If the expectation from an engineering perspective is you can use cursor and ship twice as much code... that's execution getting cheaper." But this creates massive demand for code review, security audits, quality assurance—not despite the speed, but because of it.

- **Customer success shifts from reactive to strategic**: As ticket-answering automates, customer success people who can "advocate for the customer internally aggressively with those pesky PMs" and "talk with sales about expansion revenue" become the valuable ones. The role inverts from customer-facing to internal-advocacy.

- **Data preparation is the bottleneck, not model capability**: "Most of the failures I see in AI projects come from the data side." Companies focus on model selection while the real work is chunking, vectorization, governance, ETL pipelines.

- **QA shifts from launch-gate to continuous guardian**: Traditional "P0, P1, P2, launch" QA doesn't work for probabilistic systems. QA must become continuous production monitoring, which is more work, not less.

- **Edge intelligence creates a new engineering discipline**: Combining robotics, inference optimization, and device constraints creates a role (edge engineers) that doesn't exist yet but will be massive.

- **The simulation economy is underpriced**: "Getting behavioral data out of simulations is going to be a big deal." Most people think about AI for prediction; Nate highlights AI-enabled simulation to generate training data and test scenarios.

## Strategic Patterns Identified

1. **The Beating Heart Pattern**: Every role has a core that AI cannot replace—the fundamental human accountability, relationship, or judgment. Strategic workforce planning identifies this core and invests there while automating the periphery. Misidentifying the beating heart leads to hollowing out valuable roles or protecting roles that should evolve.

2. **Problem-Following Value Creation**: Jobs and competitive advantage emerge where AI creates problems (quality, security, coordination, trust), not where AI provides solutions. This inverts typical technology analysis from "what can the technology do?" to "what problems does widespread adoption create?" Applied across industries, this predicts specialist demand.

3. **The Accountability Premium in Automated Systems**: As systems become more complex and automated, clear human accountability points become more valuable, not less. This creates a paradox: AI enables larger spans of control, but also requires stronger accountability. Companies that strengthen accountability while deploying AI outcompete those that see automation as replacing accountability.
