# How Jensen Works - The Nvidia Way

## Summary
An operational blueprint of how Jensen Huang built and runs Nvidia over 31+ years as the longest-running founder-CEO of a tech company. The analysis strips away biography to focus purely on Jensen's 20+ operational ideas: flat organization with 60 direct reports, public criticism as learning tool, "speed of light" as only benchmark, "tortured into greatness" through relentless self-criticism, swarming greatest opportunities over decades. The core philosophy: fight complacency through constant reinvention, hire the best and choke them with gold, and make suffering your competitive advantage. Jensen's approach is unapologetically extreme in all things - work ethic, standards, directness, and time horizon.

## 1. Context

**Background:**
Jensen Huang co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and has served as CEO for 31+ years, making him the longest-running founder-CEO of a tech company in the world. The video is based on "The Nvidia Way" by Tay Kim, focusing exclusively on operational ideas - "how Jensen works and nothing else." At the time of certain stories, Intel was 860x larger than Nvidia in revenue, yet Jensen declared "we need to kill Intel." Today, Nvidia is a multi-trillion dollar company at the center of the AI revolution.

**Why This Matters:**
This is a rare deep-dive into the operational philosophy of a founder who built something extraordinary over three decades. Jensen's ideas aren't theoretical - they're battle-tested through multiple near-death experiences, market crashes (80% stock decline in 2008), and the transformation from graphics cards to AI infrastructure. His approach demonstrates how extreme, consistent operational discipline compounds into competitive advantage.

**Key Stats:**
- 31+ years as founder-CEO (longest in tech)
- 60 direct reports (no one-on-ones)
- Reads 100+ "top five" emails daily from employees
- Stock fell 80% during 2008 crisis while he invested heavily in CUDA
- Intel was 860x larger than Nvidia when Jensen said "we need to kill Intel" (1997)
- Gross margin fell from 45% to 35% due to CUDA investment (2007-2008)
- Multi-trillion dollar company today

## 2. Vision & Why

**Core Mission:**
Build a company that naturally attracts amazing people and enables them to work at the speed of light - contained only by the laws of physics. Create markets that don't exist rather than fight for share in existing markets. The underlying mission: continuous reinvention as mandatory, not optional.

**The "Why" Behind It:**
Jensen believes Nvidia's worst enemy is not competition but itself - specifically, the complacency that grips successful companies. His inner voice tells him "you suck" even after blowing out a quarter. This drives a philosophy that nothing is ever good enough, excellence requires suffering, and greatness comes from character formed through pain, not intelligence.

**Enduring Nature:**
The anti-complacency mindset remained constant for 31+ years. Every monthly meeting in the early days: "We're 30 days from going out of business." Even at peak success, Jensen says he looks in the mirror every morning and says "you suck." The vision evolved from graphics to general computing to AI, but the underlying philosophy of paranoid urgency never changed.

---

## 3. Strategic Engine

**How They Actually Won:**
Jensen built a self-reinforcing system with three core components: (1) Flat organization with radical transparency enabling fast information flow and decision-making, (2) Public criticism as organizational learning mechanism that refines company character, (3) Long-term, heavy investment in market creation (CUDA) despite 80% stock crashes and investor pressure, creating 15+ year moats through ecosystem lock-in.

**Key Components:**
1. **Organizational Design:** 60 direct reports, no one-on-ones, flat structure that attracts top talent and repels those who can't think independently
2. **Information Architecture:** "Top Five" emails from every level (reads 100/day), whiteboard-first meetings for transparent thinking, unfiltered information "from the edge"
3. **Investment Philosophy:** "Swarm your greatest opportunity" - identified GPU computing for non-graphics use in 2002, invested heavily 2007-2008 despite crisis, educated entire market (wrote textbooks, 100+ talks), 15+ year compounding period
4. **Talent System:** "Choke you with gold" - special stock grants in the moment, direct awards from CEO to any level, blood-like protection of equity allocation
5. **Speed Culture:** "Speed of light" benchmark - judge against theoretical maximum (no delays, no queues), not against past performance or competitors

**Why This Worked:**
The system creates compounding advantages: flat structure + public learning = faster iteration cycles. Heavy ecosystem investment (CUDA) + market education = high switching costs. Extreme talent compensation + mission-focused structure = retention of world-class performers. Speed culture + anti-complacency = continuous reinvention before disruption arrives. Each component reinforces others, making the system hard to copy without accepting the full pain of the approach.

## 4. Culture & Incentives

**Cultural Principles:**
- **Complacency kills:** Treated as the deadliest sin. Jensen: "At the height of success, you're most vulnerable to complacency."
- **Pain builds character:** "Excellence is the capacity to take pain." Jensen: "My ability to endure pain and suffering is my superpower."
- **No rearview mirror:** Make something great, then immediately do the next thing. Don't dwell on wins.
- **Tortured into greatness:** "I don't like giving up on people. I'd rather torture them into greatness."
- **Public accountability:** "We're not optimizing for not embarrassing somebody. We're optimizing for the company learning from our mistakes."
- **Mission is the boss:** Not serving an organization, serving the mission itself.
- **Only the paranoid survive:** Borrowed from Andy Grove, embedded in daily operations.

**Incentive Structure:**
- Stock treated "like blood" - Jensen pours over allocation reports personally
- Special one-off grants awarded in the moment for extraordinary contributions (email: "Special grant in recognition of your extraordinary contributions")
- CEO can reach down to any level and award stock directly without waiting for annual review
- Clear description of rationale behind every award
- 4-year vesting on special grants
- Managers can refer employees for special consideration to senior executives
- No traditional one-on-ones means performance feedback is immediate and public

**Alignment Mechanisms:**
- "Top Five" emails with action words (finalize, build, secure) tagged by topic - creates direct CEO-to-edge communication
- Whiteboard-first meetings force transparent thinking in real-time
- Public criticism means entire organization learns from single mistakes
- Flat structure with 60 direct reports ensures no information filtering
- "Pilot in command" for every project - always a name, never "such and such team"
- Collective information sharing across executive staff - everyone knows what everyone else is doing

## 5. Resource & Capital Allocation

**Where Money Flowed:**
- **CUDA Development (2007-2008):** So heavy that gross margin fell from 45% to 35% during the worst possible time (global financial crisis, stock down 80%)
- **Market Education:** Wrote textbooks, gave 100+ talks per year, provided CUDA machines to universities if they'd teach classes, traveling road show
- **Talent Compensation:** Special stock grants awarded in the moment, willing to massively overpay for world-class performers
- **R&D for Market Creation:** Focus on "0-to-1" opportunities where there are no customers (and therefore no competitors)

**What They DIDN'T Spend On:**
- Traditional hierarchy/management layers - refused to hire COO despite board recommendations
- Commodity products - Jensen: "We should be doing things that other people cannot"
- Five-year planning processes - "We just plan continuously"
- One-on-one meetings - Jensen: "Doesn't fit my management philosophy"
- Fighting for market share in existing markets - "We would rather create the market"

**Allocation Philosophy:**
"I have a very clear priority list and I start from the highest priority work first. Before I even get to work, my day is already a success. I've already completed my most important work." Combined with: "There are only a handful of things that are really important and you should devote all your time to those and forget everything else." When conviction exists about a market opportunity (CUDA, AI), allocate massively and hold through pain - 15+ year time horizon.

## 6. Moats & Time Horizon

**Competitive Advantages:**
1. **Ecosystem Lock-In (CUDA)** - 15+ year head start on GPU programming model, tens of thousands trained, became industry standard. Durability: 10+ years and counting.
2. **Network Effects** - "The more people who had CUDA in their hands, the faster technology would establish itself as a standard. We should push this everywhere and make it a foundational technology."
3. **Talent Density** - 60 direct reports who are "world class at what they do and do it better than I do. Much better than I do." Attracts more top talent.
4. **Speed Culture** - "Speed of light" benchmark means faster iteration cycles than competitors using normal benchmarks (past performance or competitive set).
5. **Market Creation Expertise** - Track record of identifying "$0 markets" (robotics: "sure there are no customers") 10-20 years early and educating entire markets.
6. **Organizational Design** - Flat structure with radical transparency is extremely hard to copy without accepting the full pain of public criticism and no traditional management.

**Time Horizon:**
**Long-term (10+ years) to Forever** - The CUDA story demonstrates the time horizon: first reference to GPU computing for non-graphics in 2002 research paper. Heavy investment 2007-2008. Became trillion-dollar advantage 15+ years later. Jensen: "All of the things that I value most about my abilities. Intelligence is not at the top of the list. One of the things that I values most is the ability to work on something for a very very long period of time."

**Why Time Was Their Friend:**
CUDA ecosystem compounded daily - every new developer trained, every new course taught, every new application built increased switching costs. Competitors couldn't shortcut the ecosystem development even with better technology. Jensen: "I had a deep belief in its potential. We are convinced that accelerated computing would solve problems that normal computers couldn't. We had to make that sacrifice." Holding through 80% stock decline while investing in moat-building separated Nvidia from everyone else.

## 7. Flywheels & Lock-In

**Primary Flywheel:**
The CUDA Ecosystem Flywheel that turned Nvidia from graphics company into AI infrastructure platform

**Flywheel Visualization:**
```
[1. Build GPU with programmable layer (CUDA)]
→ [2. Make it easier to program than competitors]
→ [3. Educate market (textbooks, talks, university courses)]
→ [4. More developers learn CUDA]
→ [5. More applications built on CUDA]
→ [6. Higher demand for Nvidia GPUs]
→ [7. More resources to improve CUDA and GPU performance]
→ [8. Larger ecosystem attracts more developers]
→ [Back to Step 4, exponentially stronger]
```

**Lock-In Mechanisms:**
- **Developer Training:** Wrote and distributed textbooks translated into multiple languages, used by hundreds of schools. Chief scientist gave 100+ talks in one year, taught university classes.
- **Code Investment:** Every application built on CUDA represents switching cost. Researchers reported "significant speed improvements" which became performance benchmarks.
- **Skill Scarcity:** CUDA skills are specialized. Developers trained in CUDA ecosystem have strong preference to stay in ecosystem.
- **Network Effects:** "GPGPU had the potential to open up the market for GPUs far beyond mere computer graphics." The more applications, the more valuable the platform.
- **First Mover Advantage:** "Where there are no customers, there are also no competitors. That is a $0 billion market."

**Compounding Effect:**
Started 2002-2007 (research phase), heavy investment 2007-2008 (crisis period), 2008-2015 (ecosystem building), 2015+ (AI explosion). 15+ years of compounding. By the time competitors saw the opportunity, Nvidia had insurmountable ecosystem advantage. Jensen's willingness to hold through 80% stock decline and investor pressure ("demanded a strategic course correction") while investing in the moat is what made it unreplicable.

## 8. Stakeholder Alignment

**Winners (Win-Win-Win):**
- **Top Employees:** "Choke you with gold" compensation, special grants in the moment, direct CEO recognition, flat structure enables faster career impact
- **Developers/Researchers:** Free or subsidized CUDA machines to universities, free textbooks, free training, careers built on cutting-edge technology
- **Customers:** Products with "radically different" performance, pricing power offset by genuine technological leaps ("as long as they look at the screen and see something radically different, they're going to buy it")
- **Shareholders (long-term):** Multi-trillion dollar value creation, but requires stomaching 80% drawdowns and heavy margin compression
- **Academic Institutions:** Free equipment, curriculum support, textbooks, access to cutting-edge research

**Losers:**
- **Employees Who Can't Handle Public Criticism:** "A flat structure fights against the danger of slow decision-making" but "would weed out lower performers who were unaccustomed to thinking for themselves"
- **Employees Seeking Work-Life Balance:** "People who train for the Olympics grumble about training early in the morning, too. Long hours were a necessary prerequisite for excellence."
- **Competitors (Intel, others):** Jensen in 1997: "We need to kill Intel." Nvidia's ecosystem strategy and willingness to price low-end chips at a loss locked competitors out.
- **Employees Who Want Career Coaching:** Jensen "did not want to spend time on career coaching because the majority of them had already reached the pinnacle of their careers"
- **Traditional Middle Managers:** No room for them in flat structure with 60 direct reports

**Ethical Considerations:**
The "tortured into greatness" approach and public criticism culture is brutal and not for everyone. Jensen is explicit about this: "I don't like giving up on people. I'd rather torture them into greatness." The work intensity ("if I'm not working, I'm thinking about working") sets expectations that filter for extreme performers but may be unsustainable for many. However, he applies the same standard to himself: "I look in the mirror every morning and say 'You suck.'"

## 9. North Star Metric

**What They Optimized For:**
**Speed of Execution (Speed of Light)** - Not revenue, not market share, not margins in isolation. The benchmark is: "What is the theoretical maximum speed possible if there are no delays, no queues, and no downtime?" Jensen: "We will judge ourselves against the speed of light, not what we used to do or what other companies are doing."

**Why This Metric:**
Speed is the meta-metric that enables everything else: faster iteration = faster learning, faster learning = better products, better products = market creation, market creation = moats. Speed also fights complacency - there's no "good enough" when benchmark is theoretical maximum. As he observed at other companies: "internal rot" comes from slowing down and judging against yesterday's performance rather than what's physically possible.

**How They Measured:**
Project-by-project: "Each project must be broken down into component tasks and each task must have a target time to completion that assumes no delays, no queues and no downtime. This sets the theoretical maximum, the speed of light that it is physically impossible to exceed." Then measure actual performance against this theoretical maximum. Deviation from speed of light indicates inefficiency, bureaucracy, or lack of urgency - all symptoms of complacency.

### Non-Obvious Insights

- **Teaching as Primary CEO Function:** Jensen is called "Professor Jensen" because colleagues see him as spending most of his time teaching. The "Vulcan mindmeld" effect where unrelated employees say the same things demonstrates that teaching scales culture. This connects to Jim Sinegal's "90% of your time teaching" philosophy.

- **Whiteboard as Cultural Artifact:** The whiteboard isn't just a communication tool - it's a philosophy of impermanence. "The whiteboard represents both possibility and ephemerality. The belief that a successful idea, no matter how brilliant, must eventually be erased and a new one must take its place." This physical object embeds the anti-complacency mindset.

- **Public Criticism as Organizational Learning:** Most companies follow "praise publicly, criticize privately." Jensen inverts this deliberately. The logic: "Feedback is learning. For what reason are you the only person who should learn from this? We should all learn from that opportunity." This scales learning across the organization and creates accountability culture.

- **Self-Criticism Precedes Criticizing Others:** The "tortured into greatness" approach starts with Jensen himself. After "blowing the doors off the quarter," he says "I look in the mirror every morning and say 'You suck.'" The external pressure on the organization mirrors his internal pressure on himself.

- **Flat Structure as Filtering Mechanism:** 60 direct reports with no one-on-ones isn't chaos - it's a deliberate filter. "A flat structure would weed out lower performers who were unaccustomed to thinking for themselves and to acting without being told what to do." The structure selects for independence and world-class capability.

- **Speed of Light as Anti-Complacency Tool:** Benchmarking against theoretical maximum (physics-constrained perfection) rather than past performance or competitors is a brilliant defense against complacency. You can never be satisfied because the gap between actual and theoretical maximum is always visible.

- **Mission is Boss Enables Radical Flexibility:** By making the mission (not the org chart) the boss, Jensen can "take the people that we have and redirect them into a new mission." This enables the rapid pivots needed for continuous reinvention.

- **Top Five Emails Create Unfiltered Information Flow:** Reading 100 emails daily from all levels gives Jensen "information from the edge" - weak signals before they become strong. This prevents the information filtering that happens in traditional hierarchies. "Strategy isn't what I say, it's what they do."

- **Pilot in Command Prevents Hiding:** Every project must have a name attached, never "such and such team is working on that." This is identical to Elon's algorithm: "You need to know the name of the real person who made that requirement." Accountability requires individual humans, not departments.

- **Ship the Whole Cow as Market Defense:** Using "failed" chips (didn't meet quality standards) to create low-end product line is defensive genius. It locks competitors out of the low end while costing Nvidia almost nothing (no R&D, just repackaging). Can price at a loss to competitors while still profitable.

- **Market Creation Requires Market Education:** When you invent a new market (CUDA, instant photography), the market is confused. Solution: massive education investment. Chief scientist gave 100+ talks in a year, taught university classes, wrote textbooks. Not just product development but market development.

- **Suffering as Competitive Advantage:** "People with high expectations have very low resilience. And resilience matters in success. I hope suffering happens to you." The counter-intuitive insight: suffering early in life/career creates resilience that becomes competitive advantage later. It's not about intelligence.

- **Work Priority Before Work:** "Before I even get to work, my day is already a success. I've already completed my most important work." The highest priority work happens before the workday begins. This ensures important work doesn't get crowded out by urgent work.

- **Hold Through 80% Decline While Investing in Moat:** The real test of conviction isn't talking about long-term thinking - it's continuing to invest in the moat (CUDA) when stock is down 80%, margins are compressed 10 points, and investors demand course correction. This is what separates Nvidia from everyone else.

- **$0 Markets Have No Competitors:** "Where there are no customers, there are also no competitors." The insight behind market creation strategy. By targeting markets that barely exist or don't exist at all, you can build a monopoly position before anyone realizes there's a market.

- **Champions Behave Like Champions Before They're Champions:** Mitch Rales taking pages of notes despite being most successful person in the room. Jensen showing up to academic conferences to learn when he wasn't scheduled to speak. The habits that make you successful are the same habits you practice before success arrives.

## 11. Application & Mental Model

### When to Use This Pattern

**Signals this approach is relevant:**
- You're in a rapidly evolving industry where yesterday's winners become tomorrow's losers (tech, AI, biotech)
- You have a 10+ year time horizon and patient capital that can stomach volatility
- You're trying to create a new market or category, not fight for share in existing market
- Speed of iteration and learning matters more than process efficiency
- You need to attract and retain world-class talent who won't tolerate bureaucracy
- The competitive advantage comes from ecosystem/network effects (platform, developer tools, infrastructure)
- You see a weak signal that others are missing and you have deep conviction
- Your biggest risk is complacency/internal rot, not external competition
- You personally can model extreme work ethic and resilience (the torture starts with you)

### When NOT to Use This Pattern

**Conditions where this backfires:**
- Mature, stable industry where execution on known playbook matters more than reinvention
- Regulated industry where compliance documentation and process controls are mandatory
- Business with high employee turnover costs where the "flat structure filtering mechanism" would be too expensive
- Consumer brand where consistency and predictability are the value proposition
- Short-term capital (need to show steady quarterly results, can't hold through 80% decline)
- Limited talent pool - can't hire only world-class performers who can operate independently
- Founder/CEO doesn't personally embody extreme work ethic (hypocrisy would kill culture)
- Legal/HR risk from public criticism culture outweighs learning benefits
- Team that needs career development coaching (Jensen explicitly doesn't do this)
- Partnership-based model where preserving relationships matters more than speed/truth

### How to Apply to 1658 Holdings Companies

**Finland DMC Oy (Tourism/DMC):**
- **Anti-Complacency Rituals:** Monthly review asking "what would a competitor do to put us out of business?" Force continuous reinvention even at peak success.
- **Flat Information Flow:** "Top Five" email system from tour guides and operations staff directly to leadership. Don't filter insights through management layers.
- **Speed of Light Benchmark:** What's the theoretical fastest time to respond to customer inquiry, design custom tour, resolve issue? Measure against that, not last year's performance.
- **Expected Outcome:** Faster iteration on product offerings, earlier identification of market trends (weak signals from edge), culture of urgency despite market leadership.

**General Principles for Portfolio Companies:**

1. **Teaching as Leadership:** CEOs should spend 50%+ of time teaching the organization. Strategy spreads through teaching, not memos. Create "Professor [CEO Name]" reputation internally.

2. **Public Learning from Mistakes:** Consider shifting some criticism from private to public (team meetings, all-hands) when the learning value is high. Frame it as "company learning" not personal attack.

3. **Mission is Boss:** For major initiatives, assign "Pilot in Command" with direct CEO reporting line. Remove matrix management friction. Name must be attached to every project.

4. **Swarm Your Greatest Opportunity:** When you identify the 10-20 year opportunity, allocate massively even if current metrics suffer. Hold through volatility. Nvidia's CUDA investment (2007-2008) destroyed margins during a crisis but created trillion-dollar moat.

5. **Speed Benchmark vs. Theoretical Maximum:** Stop benchmarking against last year or competitors. Ask: "What's physically possible with no delays or friction?" That's your benchmark. Gap between actual and possible reveals inefficiency.

6. **Direct Information Flow:** Create mechanisms for unfiltered information from edge to center. "Top Five" emails, skip-level connections, CEO customer calls. Strategy is what they do, not what you say.

7. **Character Over Intelligence in Hiring:** Look for resilience and pain tolerance, not just credentials. Jensen: "Greatness comes from character. And character is formed out of people who suffered."

8. **Market Creation Over Market Share:** Before fighting for share in existing market, ask: "Can we create a new market category where there are no competitors?" $0 markets become $X billion markets.

9. **Hold Your Convictions:** If you have deep conviction about a strategic bet, hold through the pain. Nvidia held CUDA through 80% stock decline and investor pressure for course correction. That's what made the moat unreplicable.

10. **Work Your Highest Priority First:** Complete most important work before the workday begins. Don't let urgent crowd out important. "Before I even get to work, my day is already a success."

### Personal Reflections
The most striking insight is how integrated Jensen's system is - you can't copy one piece without the others. Public criticism only works with flat structure (no hiding behind layers). Flat structure only works if you attract A+ players. A+ players only stay if compensated extraordinarily well ("choke with gold"). Extraordinary compensation only makes sense if stock price compounds over decades. Stock compounds if you build moats. Moats require holding through 80% declines while investing. Holding through pain requires conviction. Conviction comes from "information from the edge" and deep involvement.

The second-order insight: this system is deliberately designed to be unreplicable by normal companies. Most companies can't tolerate 80% stock declines. Most CEOs can't handle 60 direct reports. Most cultures reject public criticism. Most boards won't let CEO reject COO hire. Jensen built a system that works precisely because it's so painful that competitors can't copy it. The pain IS the moat.

The third-order insight: "I hope suffering happens to you" is the most important career advice in the entire transcript. Resilience and pain tolerance aren't teachable in business school - they come from actual suffering. This explains why many second-generation wealthy kids struggle in business (no suffering) and why many immigrant entrepreneurs succeed (abundant suffering). The suffering isn't a bug, it's the feature.
