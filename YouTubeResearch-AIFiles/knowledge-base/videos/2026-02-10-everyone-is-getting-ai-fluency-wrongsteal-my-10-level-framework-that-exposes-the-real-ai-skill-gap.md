# Everyone is Getting AI Fluency Wrong—Steal My 10 Level Framework That Exposes the Real AI Skill Gap

## Summary
Nate B Jones presents a 10-level AI fluency framework that moves beyond tool-specific skills to assess fundamental capabilities. The framework reveals that 80% of users plateau at levels 1-5 (basic usage to mental models), while levels 5-7 require systems thinking, and levels 7-9 demand teaching and innovation capabilities. The strategic insight: AI fluency isn't about mastering specific tools—it's about developing transferable mental models, systematic approaches, and the ability to discover undocumented capabilities. This creates a durable competitive advantage as the technology evolves.

---

## 1. Context

**Background:** Organizations struggle to assess AI competency because existing frameworks focus on tool-specific skills (ChatGPT prompts, Copilot usage) rather than transferable principles. As AI capabilities rapidly evolve, skill assessments become obsolete within months. The speaker addresses a fundamental question: "How do I level up in AI, and how do I know I'm improving?" without creating a framework tied to specific models or tools.

**Why This Matters:** For business leaders, understanding AI fluency levels enables:
- Accurate assessment of team capabilities
- Strategic hiring and development decisions
- Identification of capability gaps before they become competitive disadvantages
- Resource allocation for training based on actual skill requirements
- Recognition that "baseline" competency is shifting rapidly upward

**Key Stats:**
- 80% of users operate at levels 1-5 (basic usage through mental models)
- Most people end up below level 5 on this "tough scale"
- The competitive baseline is shifting: "the whole population is going to grow into one to three in the next year"
- Timeline warning: "It's never going to go slower than it's going right now" (regarding AI adoption pace)
- Framework spans October 2025 into 2026, acknowledging rapid evolution

## 2. Vision & Why

**Core Mission:** Create an evergreen, model-agnostic framework for assessing and developing AI fluency that remains relevant as specific tools and models evolve. The mission is to provide "a living breathing framework that helps you make sense of your own skill level relative to where we are with AI that you can come back to again and again."

**The "Why" Behind It:**
1. **Lack of Comprehensive Assessment:** "There hasn't really been a comprehensive approach that is agnostic of models, that doesn't care if you're a Chad GPT user or a Copilot user or a Claude user. It just focuses on the principles and your level of understanding."

2. **Wrong Teaching Approach:** "I have a strong conviction that AI is a generalist skill set and we are probably teaching it wrong if we dive too deep into verticals without that generalist conceptual foundation."

3. **Missing Foundation Layer:** "We really haven't had that. And that's what I'm setting out to do here. This sort of general approach to skill sets and fluency."

4. **Career Development Clarity:** Enables individuals to "understand your level and what your goals are and make sure you are equipped to get there."

**Enduring Nature:**
- **Timeless Principles:**
  - Mental models of how LLMs work (next token prediction, context retrieval)
  - Systems thinking and systematization
  - Teaching as a learning multiplier
  - Discovery-oriented innovation mindset
  
- **Time-Specific Elements (2024-2026):**
  - Specific tools mentioned (ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot)
  - Agent frameworks launched October 6th, 2025
  - Context window sizes (book-sized prompts)
  - Current capability boundaries

## 3. Strategic Engine

**How This Actually Works:**

The framework operates as a progressive capability ladder where each level builds on previous foundations while adding distinct competencies. Unlike traditional training (tool → application), this framework works backwards from outcomes:

1. **Assessment Phase:** User identifies current level based on behavioral indicators and mental model sophistication
2. **Gap Analysis:** User identifies target level based on role requirements and career goals
3. **Development Planning:** Custom 90-day plans address specific capability gaps
4. **Iterative Refinement:** Framework adapts as new AI capabilities emerge

**Key Components:**

1. **Levels 1-3: Basic to Intuitive Usage**
   - Tool familiarity (rewriting emails, adjusting documents)
   - Beginning to understand prompting patterns
   - Moving from random trial to intuitive prompt engineering

2. **Levels 3-5: Mental Model Development**
   - Understanding how LLMs actually work (next token prediction, no true "knowing")
   - Grasping context retrieval mechanisms
   - Thinking backwards from desired outputs
   - "You're going to stop asking what should I tell the AI and at this stage you're going to start asking what is the output that I need?"

3. **Levels 5-7: Systems Thinking**
   - Building auditable, repeatable patterns
   - Optimizing for "prompt yield" (quality output per unit of prompting)
   - Creating team-scalable systems
   - Having 5-7 AI tools with specific use cases
   - Maintaining prompt libraries

4. **Levels 7-9: Teaching and Innovation**
   - Documenting learning for others
   - Leading organizational AI adoption
   - Discovering undocumented AI capabilities
   - "Pull the impossible problems into the realm of the possible"

5. **Level 10: Intentionally Withheld**
   - "I don't give tens" - keeps aspiration open-ended

**Why This Works:**

1. **Model Agnostic:** Survives tool churn by focusing on transferable principles
2. **Self-Assessment Friendly:** Behavioral indicators enable honest self-evaluation
3. **Goal-Oriented:** "Not everyone has to be a 10, right? Like that's not the point."
4. **Compound Learning:** Each level creates foundation for exponential growth at next level
5. **Realistic Benchmarking:** "This is a tough scale" - sets appropriate expectations

## 4. Behavioral Design

**Behavioral Principles:**

1. **Outcome-First Thinking (Levels 3-5):**
   - Shift from "what should I tell the AI?" to "what output do I need?"
   - Mental models enable working backwards from desired results
   - "I can work back in my head" based on understanding how outputs are generated

2. **Systematic Documentation (Levels 5-7):**
   - Moving from "usually do this" to "this is the sequence I follow"
   - Creating predictable, auditable patterns
   - Building prompt libraries and tool-task associations

3. **Teaching as Learning (Levels 7-9):**
   - "Teaching has been super helpful in driving clarity and revealing gaps in my own understanding"
   - Documentation scales influence and clarifies thinking
   - "The more you document about what you're learning and what you're thinking and how you're growing, the more you're able to scale your influence"

4. **Discovery Orientation (Levels 7-9):**
   - Understanding that "AI capabilities are not all documented"
   - "We do not ship an AI and OpenAI knows all about it. We ship an AI and we all collectively discover the capabilities it has"
   - Treating AI systems as "grown" rather than "programmed"

**Incentive Structure:**

**Encourages:**
- Continuous learning through teaching
- Systems thinking over one-off solutions
- Mental model development over rote memorization
- Efficiency through prompt yield optimization
- Public documentation and knowledge sharing

**Discourages:**
- Tool-specific skill hoarding
- Inefficient trial-and-error prompting
- Staying in comfort zone levels
- Vertical specialization without horizontal foundation
- Passive consumption without production

**Alignment Mechanisms:**

1. **Self-Assessment Checkpoints:** Framework provides clear behavioral indicators for each level
2. **90-Day Development Plans:** Structured progression between levels
3. **Comprehensive Assessment Prompt:** Tool for accurate level identification
4. **Public Accountability:** Encouragement to document and share learning
5. **Shifting Baseline Warning:** "Your competitive reality is shifting" creates urgency

## 5. Time & Attention

**Where Time Flows:**

**Levels 1-3 (Basic Users - 80% of population):**
- Time spent: Learning specific tool interfaces, trial-and-error prompting
- Attention on: Immediate task completion
- Efficiency: 10 iterations to get one usable output

**Levels 3-5 (Mental Model Builders):**
- Time spent: Understanding underlying mechanisms, building intuition
- Attention on: How outputs are generated, context management
- Shift: From "what to say" to "what output needed"

**Levels 5-7 (Systems Thinkers):**
- Time spent: Creating reusable patterns, optimizing workflows
- Attention on: Prompt yield, team scalability, feedback loops
- Efficiency: "One or two prompts and get 98% of the way there and move on"
- Investment: Building prompt libraries, tool evaluation, pattern documentation

**Levels 7-9 (Teachers/Innovators):**
- Time spent: Public documentation, curriculum development, capability discovery
- Attention on: Pulling "impossible problems into the realm of the possible"
- Output: Training programs, public content, novel applications

**What This System DOESN'T Spend On:**

1. **Tool-Specific Training:** "I am not giving you the fluency levels for engineers and then the fluency levels for PMs"
2. **Vertical Specialization:** Avoiding deep-dive vertical skills without horizontal foundation
3. **Exhaustive Coverage:** "I'm not going to do all 10 because people just don't stick around for that"
4. **Perfect Prompting:** "Maybe you do [copy prompts], maybe you don't sometimes"
5. **Building AI Systems:** "None of this means you can build an AI system. None of this means that you can build a context window like a rag system or a memory system."

**Allocation Philosophy:**

"Value the tokens, value the time that we are taking with AI so that we can go on to other things. And it is much much much more efficient to just do the prompt correctly and just get the right answer."

Core principle: **Optimize for prompt yield** - maximize quality output per unit of effort invested in prompting. This creates time for higher-value activities (systems building, teaching, innovation) rather than grinding through inefficient iterations.

## 7. Flywheels & Lock-In

**Primary Flywheel: The Teaching-Learning-Systems Loop**

**Flywheel Visualization:**

[Use AI → Build Mental Models] → 
[Create Systems & Document] → 
[Teach Others / Discover Capabilities] → 
[Feedback Reveals Gaps & Novel Use Cases] → 
[Deeper Understanding & Better Systems] → 
[Back to Use AI, now at higher level]

**Detailed Flywheel Mechanics:**

1. **Initial Use (Levels 1-3):** Basic AI usage for task completion
2. **Mental Model Development (Levels 3-5):** Understanding how LLMs work enables intuitive prompting
3. **Systematization (Levels 5-7):** Patterns emerge, get documented, become repeatable
4. **Teaching Requirement (Levels 7-9):** To teach, must clarify thinking and close knowledge gaps
5. **Gap Discovery:** Teaching reveals what you don't fully understand yet
6. **Capability Discovery:** Deep usage discovers undocumented AI capabilities
7. **System Improvement:** New discoveries improve existing systems
8. **Enhanced Usage:** Return to step 1 with significantly better capabilities

**Why This Accelerates:**

- Mental models make each interaction more efficient (prompt yield improves)
- Systems create reusable assets (don't rebuild from scratch)
- Teaching forces clarity (can't teach what you don't understand)
- Discovery creates proprietary knowledge (competitive advantage)
- Better systems attract more complex problems (skill development accelerates)

**Lock-In Mechanisms:**

1. **Sunk Cost in Mental Models:**
   - "You need an overarching perspective on the fluency and competency assessment"
   - Mental models are hard-won and highly valuable

2. **Asset Accumulation:**
   - Prompt libraries specific to your workflows
   - "Five to seven tools they're working with regularly in the AI space"
   - Documented systems and patterns
   - Cost to abandon: losing organizational memory and efficiency

3. **Network Effects:**
   - "Seen by their teammates as a peer collaborator and peer leader"
   - Teaching relationships create dependencies

   - Cost to abandon: breaking team dependencies

4. **Identity Lock-In:**
   - Progression from "AI user" to "AI teacher" to "AI innovator"

   - "Documentarian" identity: "the more you document... the more you're able to scale your influence"

5. **Discovery Investment:**
   - Time spent discovering undocumented capabilities
   - Novel applications and use cases found
   - First-mover advantages on innovations
   - Cost to abandon: proprietary knowledge advantage

**Compounding Effect:**

"Most teachers will tell you regardless of subject that that's true" - referring to teaching driving learning.

The compounding happens at multiple levels simultaneously:
- **Individual:** Each level makes next level more accessible
- **Team:** Systems enable others to operate at higher levels
- **Organization:** Documentation creates institutional knowledge
- **Field:** Public teaching advances collective understanding

**Anti-Flywheel Warning:**

"The competitive baseline is shifting upward" - NOT progressing means falling behind in relative terms even if absolute skill stays constant. The flywheel must keep spinning to maintain position.

## 9. System Health Metric

**What to Optimize For: Prompt Yield**

**Definition:** "Prompt yield is like what is your quality output per unit of prompting."

**The ONE Metric:** 
**Prompt Yield = (Usable Outputs) / (Prompting Iterations)**

This metric captures:
- Efficiency of AI interaction
- Quality of mental models
- Effectiveness of systematization
- Maturity of understanding

**Why This Metric:**

1. **Spans All Levels:**
   - Level 1-3: Very low prompt yield (10 iterations → 1 output)
   - Level 3-5: Improving yield through mental models
   - Level 5-7: High yield through systems (1-2 prompts → 98% solution)
   - Level 7-9: Compound yield through teaching/discovery

2. **Captures True Value:**
   - Not about activity (number of prompts)
   - Focuses on outcomes (usable outputs)
   - "Value the tokens, value the time that we are taking with AI so that we can go on to other things"

3. **Predictive of Progression:**
   - Low yield = stuck in early levels
   - Improving yield = mental models developing
   - Consistently high yield = systems thinking achieved
   - Teaching others to achieve high yield = mastery

4. **Operationally Relevant:**
   - Directly impacts productivity
   - Scales across teams
   - Measurable and improvable
   - "Much much much more efficient to just do the prompt correctly and just get the right answer"

5. **Future-Proof:**
   - Works regardless of which AI tool
   - Remains relevant as models improve
   - Adapts to new capabilities (agents, etc.)

**Alternative/Secondary Metrics:**

While prompt yield is primary, track these at different levels:

- **Level 1-3:** Task completion time vs. baseline
- **Level 3-5:** Intuitive prompt success rate (first try)
- **Level 5-7:** Number of reusable systems/patterns created
- **Level 7-9:** Number of people taught, novel capabilities discovered

**How to Measure:**

**Individual Level:**
1. **Track Over 1 Week:**
   - Count every AI interaction
   - Mark outputs as "usable" (90%+ ready) or "unusable"
   - Count iterations required to reach usable output
   
2. **Calculate:**
   - Prompt Yield = Usable Outputs / Total Prompting Sessions
   - Target progression: 10% → 30% → 50% → 80%+

3. **Benchmark Examples:**
   - "10 iterations to get one usable output" = 10% yield
   - "One or two prompts and get 98% of the way there" = 50-100% yield

**Team Level:**
1. **System Audit:**
   - Count documented AI systems/patterns in use
   - Measure adoption rate across team
   - Track time-to-competency for new team members using systems

2. **Efficiency Delta:**
   - Compare task completion time: systematic vs. ad-hoc users
   - "Yield gap" between team members indicates training opportunities

**Organizational Level:**
1. **Capability Assessment:**
   - Percentage of team at each fluency level
   - Distribution should shift rightward over time
   - Red flag: Majority stuck below Level 5

2. **Innovation Output:**
   - Novel AI applications discovered
   - "Impossible problems" now solved
   - Indicates presence of Level 7-9 practitioners

**Warning Signals (Poor System Health):**
- Prompt yield declining over time (regression)
- Everyone stuck at same level (no progression)
- High time investment, low output quality
- No systematic documentation emerging
- No one teaching/sharing discoveries

**Health Signals:**
- Prompt yield improving week-over-week
- Team distribution shifting to higher levels
- Prompt libraries growing
- Cross-team knowledge sharing increasing
- Novel use cases emerging

It is actually meant to be a living breathing framework." The non-obvious design principle: Durability comes from appropriate abstraction level - too specific becomes obsolete, too abstract becomes useless. This framework targets mental models and behaviors that persist across tool generations.

## Strategic Patterns Identified

### Pattern 1: Capability Ladder Design
**Pattern:** Create progressive skill framework where each level builds on previous while adding distinct capabilities. Make assessment behavioral (what people do) rather than credential-based (what courses they took). Intentionally make it challenging ("tough scale") to create accurate differentiation and prevent grade inflation.

**Why It Works:** Behavioral indicators are harder to game than credentials. Progressive structure shows clear path forward while acknowledging current position. Difficulty creates signal value - everyone claiming Level 9 would make framework worthless.

**Application:** Any domain with rapidly evolving tool landscape but stable underlying principles. Could apply to: data analysis fluency, digital marketing sophistication, remote work effectiveness.

### Pattern 2: Abstraction Layer for Durability
**Pattern:** Build framework one abstraction level above specific implementations (tools) to survive churn. Focus on mental models and principles that persist across versions/vendors. Make it explicitly "model-agnostic" and "evergreen."

**Why It Works:** Tools change faster than principles. "How LLMs work" remains relevant across ChatGPT 4, 5, 6, etc. Investment in learning framework compounds rather than becoming obsolete. Creates "living breathing framework that helps you make sense of your own skill level relative to where we are with AI that you can come back to again and again."

**Application:** Any fast-moving technology domain. Could apply to: social media marketing (platforms change, audience psychology doesn't), programming languages (syntax changes, algorithms don't), software tools in general.

### Pattern 3: Teaching as Forcing Function
**Pattern:** Structure highest mastery levels around teaching/documentation requirements. Make knowledge sharing integral to advancement, not auxiliary. Use teaching to reveal gaps in understanding and accelerate learning.

**Why It Works:** "Teaching has been super helpful in driving clarity and revealing gaps in my own understanding that I have to relentlessly close. Most teachers will tell you regardless of subject that that's true." Creates virtuous cycle: teaching reveals gaps → close gaps → better teaching → more gap discovery. Also scales organizational capability through documentation.

**Application:** Any expertise domain where knowledge needs to scale. Could apply to: technical skills in growing startups, craft/trade knowledge, professional services expertise. Make "taught X people" a promotion requirement, not optional mentorship.
