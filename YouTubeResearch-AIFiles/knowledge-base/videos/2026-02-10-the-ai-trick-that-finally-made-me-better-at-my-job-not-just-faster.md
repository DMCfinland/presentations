# The AI Trick That Finally Made Me Better at My Job (Not Just Faster)

## Summary
This video presents a revolutionary framework for knowledge worker development: using AI as a personalized coach to practice discrete skills through artifact-based feedback loops. The core insight is that knowledge workers have historically lacked the deliberate practice systems that athletes and musicians use, and AI now makes it economically feasible to create structured, repeatable practice environments. By defining rubrics for work artifacts (decision docs, specs, etc.), teams can shift from abstract skill assessment to concrete, measurable improvement cycles—fundamentally changing how we hire, develop, and value talent in the AI age.

---

## 1. Context

**Background:** Inspired by Tyler Cowen's 2019 observation that "athletes train and musicians train, performers train, but knowledge workers really don't train," this video addresses a fundamental gap in professional development. Knowledge workers spend ~95% of their time in "live performance" mode—practicing their craft in front of stakeholders with real consequences—rather than in low-stakes practice environments. The emergence of capable AI models (Claude, ChatGPT) now enables structured feedback at scale for the first time.

**Why This Matters:** This is strategically relevant because it addresses three converging trends: (1) AI is commoditizing execution speed, making skill differentiation more critical; (2) the job market is shifting from role-based to skills-based assessment; (3) traditional performance management fails to develop concrete capabilities. For 1658 Holdings, this framework offers a systematic approach to accelerate talent development and create measurable competitive advantage through superior skill execution.

**Key Stats:**
- ~64% of AI usage is "shadow AI" (unreported by employees)
- ~95% of knowledge worker "reps" happen in live, high-stakes situations
- Zero structured practice environment exists for most knowledge work roles

## 2. Vision & Why

**Core Mission:** Transform knowledge work from abstract role performance to concrete skill mastery by creating practice systems that make invisible thinking patterns visible and improvable.

**The "Why" Behind It:** Three structural problems prevent knowledge worker improvement:
1. **Fuzzy outcomes** - Unlike a basketball going in the hoop, success in strategy/product/engineering is multi-dimensional and ambiguous
2. **Delayed and noisy feedback** - Decisions made in Q1 show results in Q3, contaminated by market changes, personnel shifts, and other variables
3. **Low repetition** - How many "truly consequential decision docs" does a PM write per quarter? Far too few to develop pattern recognition

This approach solves these by creating low-stakes environments with rapid, structured feedback on repeatable artifacts.

**Enduring Nature:**
- **Timeless:** The need for deliberate practice, pattern recognition development, and feedback loops
- **Timeless:** The five core skills (judgment, orchestration, coordination, taste, updating)
- **2024-2026 specific:** AI as the scalable feedback mechanism; the economic feasibility of personalized coaching
- **2024-2026 specific:** The urgency as AI commoditizes execution, making skill differentiation critical

## 3. Strategic Engine

**How This Actually Works:**
The system operates on a simple loop: define excellence → practice → get feedback → iterate. Specifically:
1. Identify a recurring artifact that matters (decision docs, specs, updates)
2. Interview trusted experts to define what "good" looks like in concrete terms
3. Create a rubric (1-5 scale) for each dimension of quality
4. Annotate 3-5 real examples with scores and rationale
5. Give this rubric + examples to an LLM as a consistent scoring system
6. Practice creating/improving artifacts, receive AI critique, identify gaps
7. Log patterns over time to track skill progression

**Key Components:**
1. **Artifact Selection** - Choosing work products that recur and matter (not one-offs)
2. **Rubric Definition** - Converting tacit knowledge into explicit scoring criteria
3. **Annotated Examples** - Red-penned docs showing what good/bad looks like concretely
4. **AI Feedback Loop** - LLM applies rubric consistently, quotes specific passages, suggests edits
5. **Practice Drills** - Low-stakes exercises (10-minute timers) focused on sub-skills

**Why This Works:**
- **Makes the invisible visible** - Thinking patterns show up in artifacts, making them coachable
- **Removes economic constraint** - AI coaching costs pennies vs. $200+/hr for human coaches
- **Enables repetition** - Can practice daily/weekly vs. quarterly in real work
- **Provides consistent feedback** - AI applies rubric identically every time, removing human variability
- **Fractal skill structure** - Complex skills (judgment) decompose into sub-skills (framing decisions, defining options, surfacing risk) that can be practiced independently

## 4. Behavioral Design

**Behavioral Principles:**
1. **Specificity over abstraction** - "Strategic thinking" is unmeasurable; "surfaces 2+ real options with explicit trade-offs" is concrete
2. **Low-stakes practice spaces** - Psychological safety to fail without career consequences
3. **Immediate feedback** - AI critique within seconds vs. waiting for quarterly reviews
4. **Visible progress** - Score tracking shows improvement trajectory, creating motivation
5. **Shared language** - Team rubrics create common vocabulary for quality discussions

**Incentive Structure:**
- **Encouraged:** Transparent AI usage, iterative improvement, seeking feedback before human review
- **Discouraged:** Hiding AI dependency, one-shot perfectionism, vague skill claims
- **Neutral:** The specific tools used (the system works with any LLM)

**Alignment Mechanisms:**
- Rubrics align team on "what good looks like" before anyone is evaluated
- AI pre-review reduces manager burden, allowing humans to focus on strategic coaching
- Practice drills identify growth areas without linking to compensation (psychological safety)
- Interview exercises mirror internal practice, ensuring hire/develop consistency

## 5. Time & Attention

**Where Time Flows:**
- **Individual practice:** 10-30 minutes weekly on focused skill drills
- **Rubric development:** 2-4 hours upfront per artifact type (one-time investment)
- **AI setup:** 30-60 minutes configuring prompts with examples
- **Team practice:** 10 minutes once or twice weekly for shared skill building
- **Human review:** Reduced time as AI pre-filters quality issues

**What This System DOESN'T Spend On:**
- Generic "leadership training" workshops disconnected from actual work
- Lengthy performance review debates about abstract qualities
- Hiring processes focused on storytelling vs. artifact demonstration
- Expensive executive coaching that doesn't scale beyond senior leaders
- Trial-and-error learning in high-stakes live situations

**Allocation Philosophy:**
"Practice small, specific skills frequently in low-stakes environments rather than practicing rarely in high-stakes environments." The system front-loads investment in defining quality (rubric creation) to enable cheap, scalable practice forever after. Time shifts from post-mortems analyzing what went wrong to pre-mortems practicing what could go right.

## 9. System Health Metric

**What to Optimize For:**
**"Artifact Quality Trajectory"** - The slope of improvement in rubric scores over time for each individual and the team average, specifically measuring: Does quality increase while revision cycles decrease?

**Why This Metric:**
This captures the core promise: people getting genuinely better, not just faster. If scores improve but revision cycles stay high, people are just gaming the rubric. If scores stay flat but revision cycles decrease, people are just getting faster at mediocrity. The magic is when both improve together—quality rises AND efficiency increases—which signals true skill development.

Secondary indicators:
- Percentage of docs passing AI rubric on first submission (efficiency)
- Time-to-decision reduction (speed)
- Correlation between rubric scores and real-world outcomes (validation)
- Team member self-reported confidence levels (psychological signal)

**How to Measure:**
1. **Log rubric scores** for each artifact produced (decision docs, specs, etc.)
2. **Track revision cycles** - How many rounds before stakeholder approval?
3. **Plot trends** - Individual and team score trajectories over quarters
4. **Sample quality checks** - Periodically have humans verify AI scoring accuracy
5. **Outcome correlation** - Retrospectively link high-scoring artifacts to business results

Practical implementation:
- Simple spreadsheet: Date | Artifact Type | Scores by Dimension | Revision Count | Outcome
- Quarterly review: Plot trend lines, identify outliers, refine rubrics
- Team discussion: "Which dimensions are we collectively weak on? What should we practice next quarter?"

### Non-Obvious Insights

- **Software embeds job-centric thinking:** Hiring and compensation tools start with job titles, literally preventing us from imagining skills independent of roles—the infrastructure itself prevents the mental shift we need.

- **Fuzzy outcomes as feature, not bug:** The ambiguity of knowledge work outcomes (speed? quality? politics? relationships?) isn't a measurement problem to solve but a design challenge requiring multi-dimensional rubrics.

- **Practice requires permission to fail:** The reason knowledge workers don't practice isn't laziness—it's structural. Our environment punishes failure in live situations, so we never create low-stakes practice spaces.

- **Rubrics are capture mechanisms:** The act of defining what "good" looks like converts tacit expert knowledge into institutional assets that outlive individual experts.

- **AI makes human coaching economics work:** The bottleneck was never knowing what to practice (experts could always articulate this) but the cost of applying that knowledge consistently at scale.

- **Hiring and development pointing at different targets:** Most organizations test for different attributes in hiring than they develop post-hire, creating a disconnect. Artifact-based approaches align both.

- **Surveillance vs. development is tool-agnostic:** The same rubric system can be oppressive (every doc scored for evaluation) or developmental (practice drills not tied to compensation). The technology doesn't determine the culture.

- **Skills are fractal, not binary:** "Judgment" decomposes into "framing decisions" + "defining options" + "assessing uncertainty" + "surfacing risks," each of which can be practiced independently like piano finger exercises.

- **The real moat is institutional rubrics:** What separates great teams isn't secret tools or processes but accumulated wisdom about what quality looks like in their specific context—now capturable in executable form.

- **Quality definition precedes AI value:** You can't "AI your way to better" without first defining what "better" means. The human work of rubric creation is irreplaceable and where the real strategic value hides.

## 11. Application & Mental Model

### When to Use This Pattern

**Ideal Conditions:**
- Work output is artifact-based (documents, designs, code, plans) rather than purely interpersonal
- Quality distinctions exist but are implicit/tacit rather than explicit
- People perform similar tasks repeatedly (decision-making, spec-writing, architecture design)
- Feedback cycles are long or inconsistent in current state
- Team wants to improve but lacks shared language for "good"
- AI tools are available and culturally acceptable
- Management supports practice time without immediate ROI pressure

**Signal Indicators:**
- Frequent debates about abstract qualities ("Was that strategic?")
- High revision cycles before stakeholder approval
- New hires struggle to ramp because "culture" isn't explained
- Performance reviews feel subjective and contentious
- Shadow AI usage is high because people fear being judged for AI assistance
- Teams want to level up but don't know how

### When NOT to Use This Pattern

**Inappropriate Conditions:**
- Work is purely interpersonal (therapy, coaching, sales relationships) with minimal artifacts
- Quality is genuinely subjective with no expert consensus possible
- Tasks are one-off creative efforts rather than repeatable patterns
- Organization culture punishes transparency or failure
- Management would weaponize scoring for evaluation rather than development
- AI literacy is too low and training would be prohibitive
- Time horizons are too short for skill compounding to matter

**Warning Signs:**
- Leaders demanding "quantified productivity metrics" for surveillance
- No psychological safety for admitting knowledge gaps
- Rapid churn making skill investment non-viable
- Work context changes so fast that rubrics become obsolete immediately
- Attempting this during crisis/turnaround when firefighting dominates

### How to Apply to 1658 Holdings Companies

**Finland DMC Oy:**
- **Client proposal quality rubric** - Define what makes a winning DMC proposal: specificity of logistics, cultural authenticity, risk mitigation, pricing transparency, creative differentiation. Practice proposals for fictional clients weekly, get AI feedback, track how win rates correlate with rubric scores.
- **Expected outcome:** Higher proposal win rates, faster new sales hire ramps, consistent quality across team, captured expertise from senior team members who've done this for decades.

- **Itinerary design rubric** - What makes an excellent Finland itinerary? Balance of experiences, logistical flow, cultural depth, flexibility provisions, local insider elements. Create practice drills: "Here's a 5-day request with budget X and interests Y—design it in 20 minutes." AI scores against rubric.
- **Expected outcome:** Junior planners produce senior-quality work faster, clients comment on higher satisfaction, less back-and-forth revision.

- **Crisis response documentation** - When travel disruptions happen (weather, strikes), what does excellent client communication look like? Speed, clarity, options presented, empathy, solution orientation. Practice responding to simulated crises.
- **Expected outcome:** Better client retention during inevitable disruptions, team confidence in handling problems, competitive advantage in service quality.

**General Principles:**

1. **Start with one high-leverage artifact**
Don't boil the ocean. Pick the single work product where quality variance most impacts outcomes. For most knowledge businesses, this is either: (1) the thing you sell (proposals, designs, strategies), or (2) the thing that coordinates everyone else (specs, decisions, plans). Build one excellent rubric before scaling.

2. **Extract tacit knowledge from your best performers**
Your top people can't usually articulate why they're good—it's intuitive. The rubric process forces externalization. Spend serious time (4-6 hours) with them marking up examples until you have 10-15 concrete criteria. This is gold mining: you're extracting years of pattern recognition into executable form.

3. **Separate practice from evaluation**
If people think scores affect compensation, the system fails immediately. Practice must be psychologically safe. Make this explicit: "We're logging scores to track improvement, not to determine bonuses." Consider keeping practice scores private to individuals unless they choose to share.

4. **Use hiring as forcing function**
The fastest way to validate your rubrics: give candidate exercises, score them, hire based on results, then track if high-scorers actually perform better. This creates accountability—if your rubric doesn't predict success, it's wrong and needs refinement.

5. **Compound by teaching others**
Once someone masters a skill via this system, have them help refine the rubric and coach others. This creates a mentorship culture where improvement is expected and visible, not vague and heroic.

## Strategic Patterns Identified

1. **Artifact-Based Skill Development Pattern**
Convert invisible thinking into visible artifacts, then create feedback loops on those artifacts. This pattern applies whenever expertise is tacit but output is concrete. Examples: writing quality (rubrics for blog posts), design taste (rubrics for UI mockups), strategic thinking (rubrics for strategy docs).

2. **AI-Enabled Deliberate Practice Pattern**
Use AI's consistency and scale to provide feedback that was previously too expensive. This pattern works when: (1) quality criteria can be articulated, (2) examples exist, (3) feedback needs to be frequent and consistent, (4) human expert time is the bottleneck. The AI doesn't need to be perfect—it needs to be consistent and good enough to drive improvement.

3. **Skills-Over-Jobs Career Framework Pattern**
Organize development around portable competencies (judgment, orchestration, taste) rather than role-specific duties (PM responsibilities, engineer expectations). This pattern prepares for a future where AI handles execution and humans differentiate on higher-order skills that compose across contexts.
