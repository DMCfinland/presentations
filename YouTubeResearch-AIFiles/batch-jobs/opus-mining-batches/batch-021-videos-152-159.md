## 9. System Health Metric

**What to Optimize For:**

**Primary Metric: Autonomous Work Duration (Human-Equivalent Hours)**

The ONE metric that matters most: **How many consecutive hours can an AI agent work on real-world tasks without human intervention, measured in human-equivalent work time?**

Specifically:
- Not wall-clock time (AI might work 60 hours in 10 hours of real time)
- But human-equivalent complexity and duration
- On economically valuable tasks (not toy problems)
- Without human supervision or correction mid-task
- Measured independently (METR standard)

**Current State:** ~2 hours (as of late 2024/early 2025)
**Projected Mid-2026:** 8 hours (full work day)
**Projected End-2027:** Exceeds human performance (not just matches duration but quality)

**Why This Metric:**

1. **Direct Economic Correlation**
   - Work duration directly translates to task delegation
   - Can measure ROI: hours delegated × average knowledge worker cost
   - Scales across industries and use cases
   - Unambiguous commercial value

2. **Hard to Game**
   - Independent measurement (METR) prevents self-reporting bias
   - Requires actual sustained performance, not cherry-picked demos
   - Can't be optimized for without genuine capability improvement
   - Real tasks, not synthetic benchmarks

3. **Predictive Power**
   - Doubling every 7 months is consistent and measurable
   - Falsifiable: can prove predictions wrong in 6-12 months
   - Creates clear timeline for planning: 15min → 2hr → 8hr → 40hr progression
   - Leading indicator for economic impact

4. **Avoids Goodhart's Law**
   - Not a public leaderboard metric (less gaming incentive)
   - Expensive to optimize for (requires actual capability)
   - Measured on diverse real tasks (can't overfit to specific test)
   - Validated through multiple independent sources (METR, GDP-val convergence)

5. **Captures What Matters**
   - Humans care about "can I delegate this task and stop thinking about it?"
   - Duration captures reliability, robustness, error recovery
   - Implicitly measures planning, world modeling, error correction
   - Reveals compound capabilities (not isolated skills)

**How to Measure:**

**For Organizations (Internal Tracking):**

1. **Baseline Assessment (Month 0)**
   - Select 10-20 representative real work tasks
   - Document typical human completion time
   - Note supervision/correction requirements
   - Establish quality standards

2. **Monthly Testing Protocol**
   - Run same tasks through current AI agents
   - Track: autonomous work time before human intervention needed
   - Measure: quality of output vs. human baseline
   - Calculate: effective work duration (quality-adjusted)

3. **Key Tracking Points**
   - Maximum autonomous duration achieved (best case)
   - Median autonomous duration (typical case)
   - Failure modes and patterns
   - Types of tasks progressing fastest

4. **Red Flags (System Unhealthy)**
   - Autonomous duration plateaus for 3+ months
   - Quality degrades even as duration increases
   - Failure modes stay constant (not improving)
   - Gaming detected (cherry-picking easier subtasks)

**For Industry (External Benchmarking):**

1. **Use METR Standard**
   - Subscribe to METR benchmark updates
   - Compare internal results to public progress
   - Validate that internal measurements track industry curve

2. **Use GDP-val for Validation**
   - Test on GDP-val task subset relevant to your industry
   - Compare to expert human performance
   - Track gap closing rate

3. **Doubling Time Check**
   - Every 7 months, autonomous duration should ~2x
   - If not, either:
     - Your measurement is wrong
     - You're behind the frontier (need better models)
     - Industry hit a wall (unlikely per Julian's thesis)

**Leading vs. Lagging Indicators:**

- **Leading:** Types of tasks becoming delegable (complexity frontier moving)
- **Coincident:** Autonomous work duration (current capability)
- **Lagging:** Economic impact, workforce changes (months behind capability)

**What NOT to Measure:**

❌ Benchmark leaderboard rankings (easily gamed)
❌ Model parameter count (not correlated with useful work)
❌ Anecdotal demo success (cherry-picking bias)
❌ Percentage scores on specific tests (Goodhart's Law)
❌ Cost per token (only matters if capability exists)
❌ Marketing claims from AI vendors (incentive misalignment)

**Practical Tracking Framework:**

```
Month 1:  Autonomous Work Duration = 0.25 hours (15 min baseline)
Month 8:  Target = 0.5 hours (first doubling)
Month 15: Target = 1 hour (second doubling)
Month 22: Target = 2 hours (third doubling) ← We are here late 2024
Month 29: Target = 4 hours (fourth doubling)
Month 36: Target = 8 hours (fifth doubling) ← Mid-2026 projection
```

If your organization is seeing this progression internally, you're tracking the frontier. If you're seeing slower progress, you're either:
1. Measuring wrong tasks (too hard for current models)
2. Using inferior models (not frontier)

  - Directly valuable (hours delegated = ROI calculable)
  - Expensive to game (requires actual capability)

**Application:** Abandon vanity metrics (test scores, parameters, benchmarks). Track autonomous work duration on real tasks. Validate with independent measurement. This prevents wasted optimization and focuses on genuine capability.

### Pattern 3: Information Asymmetry Compounding

**Core Pattern:** In rapidly evolving fields, information asymmetry between insiders and outsiders grows rather than shrinks. Insiders see 6-12 months ahead. - Frontier lab researchers see capability trajectories before public release

- This creates permanent stratification between early and late movers

**The Compounding Effect:**
```
Information → Early Action → Positioning → More Information → Better Actions → Stronger Positioning
```

**Application:** Recognize when you have information asymmetry advantages (or disadvantages). If insider: act decisively during public skepticism. If outsider: identify credible insider signals and act before public consensus. The window between insider knowledge and public knowledge is the opportunity zone.

3. **Predicts reliability:** Higher match rates correlate with fewer failures, loops, and hallucinations

**Secondary metric: Workflow Decomposition Depth**

The average number of atomic tasks identified per workflow. This indicates whether you're operating at appropriate granularity or still thinking at workflow level.

Target: Workflows should decompose to 6-12 atomic tasks on average. As the speaker notes: "Most workflows consists of like a dozen different microtasks."

**How to Measure:**

**For Task-Model Match Rate:**

Step 1: **Create task inventory**
- List all atomic tasks executed in a given time period (week/month)
- Categorize by type (cleaning data, finding context, reasoning, transforming format, etc.)
- Document which model was used for each

Step 2: **Establish optimal pairings**
- For each task type, identify the model that performed best through comparative testing
- Document evidence (speed, quality, consistency, cost)
- Update as models evolve or new models emerge

Step 3: **Calculate match rate**
```
Match Rate = (Tasks executed by optimal model) / (Total tasks executed) × 100
```

Step 4: **Track over time**
- Monitor weekly or monthly
- Target: 70%+ match rate indicates fluency
- Below 50% suggests need for more deliberate practice

**Practical tracking approach:**

**For beginners:**
- Start with single workflow
- Decompose into tasks
- Document actual vs. optimal model choice
- Track match rate just for that workflow type

**For advanced practitioners:**
- Use workflow templates with pre-assigned optimal models
- Track deviations and reasons
- Measure match rate across all work
- A/B test close calls to refine optimal pairings

**Warning signs:**
- Match rate declining: Suggests new models not integrated or decomposition quality degrading
- Match rate stuck below 60%: Indicates insufficient comparative testing or unclear task taxonomy
- High match rate but poor results: Suggests optimal pairings are wrong—need more empirical testing

**Leading indicators:**
- Time spent on deliberate comparative testing (proxy for improving match accuracy)
- Number of models in active rotation (enables better matching)

# The AI Trick That Finally Made Me Better at My Job (Not Just Faster)

## Summary
This video presents a revolutionary framework for knowledge worker development: using AI as a personalized coach to practice discrete skills through artifact-based feedback loops. The core insight is that knowledge workers have historically lacked the deliberate practice systems that athletes and musicians use, and AI now makes it economically feasible to create structured, repeatable practice environments. By defining rubrics for work artifacts (decision docs, specs, etc.), teams can shift from abstract skill assessment to concrete, measurable improvement cycles—fundamentally changing how we hire, develop, and value talent in the AI age.

**Background:** Inspired by Tyler Cowen's 2019 observation that "athletes train and musicians train, performers train, but knowledge workers really don't train," this video addresses a fundamental gap in professional development. ## 3. Strategic Engine

**How This Actually Works:**
The system operates on a simple loop: define excellence → practice → get feedback → iterate. Specifically:
1. Identify a recurring artifact that matters (decision docs, specs, updates)
2. Interview trusted experts to define what "good" looks like in concrete terms
3. Create a rubric (1-5 scale) for each dimension of quality
4. Annotate 3-5 real examples with scores and rationale
5. Give this rubric + examples to an LLM as a consistent scoring system
6. Practice creating/improving artifacts, receive AI critique, identify gaps
7. Log patterns over time to track skill progression

**Key Components:**
1. **Artifact Selection** - Choosing work products that recur and matter (not one-offs)
2. **Rubric Definition** - Converting tacit knowledge into explicit scoring criteria
3. **Annotated Examples** - Red-penned docs showing what good/bad looks like concretely
4. **AI Feedback Loop** - LLM applies rubric consistently, quotes specific passages, suggests edits
5. **Practice Drills** - Low-stakes exercises (10-minute timers) focused on sub-skills

**Why This Works:**
- **Makes the invisible visible** - Thinking patterns show up in artifacts, making them coachable
- **Removes economic constraint** - AI coaching costs pennies vs. $200+/hr for human coaches
- **Enables repetition** - Can practice daily/weekly vs. quarterly in real work
- **Provides consistent feedback** - AI applies rubric identically every time, removing human variability
- **Fractal skill structure** - Complex skills (judgment) decompose into sub-skills (framing decisions, defining options, surfacing risk) that can be practiced independently

## 9. System Health Metric

**What to Optimize For:**
**"Artifact Quality Trajectory"** - The slope of improvement in rubric scores over time for each individual and the team average, specifically measuring: Does quality increase while revision cycles decrease?

**Why This Metric:**
This captures the core promise: people getting genuinely better, not just faster. If scores improve but revision cycles stay high, people are just gaming the rubric. If scores stay flat but revision cycles decrease, people are just getting faster at mediocrity. The magic is when both improve together—quality rises AND efficiency increases—which signals true skill development.

Secondary indicators:
- Percentage of docs passing AI rubric on first submission (efficiency)
- Time-to-decision reduction (speed)
- Correlation between rubric scores and real-world outcomes (validation)
- Team member self-reported confidence levels (psychological signal)

**How to Measure:**
1. **Log rubric scores** for each artifact produced (decision docs, specs, etc.)
2. **Track revision cycles** - How many rounds before stakeholder approval?
3. **Plot trends** - Individual and team score trajectories over quarters
4. **Sample quality checks** - Periodically have humans verify AI scoring accuracy
5. **Outcome correlation** - Retrospectively link high-scoring artifacts to business results

Practical implementation:

- Quarterly review: Plot trend lines, identify outliers, refine rubrics

### How to Apply to 1658 Holdings Companies

**Finland DMC Oy:**
- **Client proposal quality rubric** - Define what makes a winning DMC proposal: specificity of logistics, cultural authenticity, risk mitigation, pricing transparency, creative differentiation. Practice proposals for fictional clients weekly, get AI feedback, track how win rates correlate with rubric scores.
- **Expected outcome:** Higher proposal win rates, faster new sales hire ramps, consistent quality across team, captured expertise from senior team members who've done this for decades.

- **Itinerary design rubric** - What makes an excellent Finland itinerary? Balance of experiences, logistical flow, cultural depth, flexibility provisions, local insider elements. - **Expected outcome:** Junior planners produce senior-quality work faster, clients comment on higher satisfaction, less back-and-forth revision.

- **Expected outcome:** Better client retention during inevitable disruptions, team confidence in handling problems, competitive advantage in service quality.

**General Principles:**

1. **Start with one high-leverage artifact**
Don't boil the ocean. Pick the single work product where quality variance most impacts outcomes. For most knowledge businesses, this is either: (1) the thing you sell (proposals, designs, strategies), or (2) the thing that coordinates everyone else (specs, decisions, plans). Build one excellent rubric before scaling.

2. **Extract tacit knowledge from your best performers**
Your top people can't usually articulate why they're good—it's intuitive. The rubric process forces externalization. Spend serious time (4-6 hours) with them marking up examples until you have 10-15 concrete criteria. This is gold mining: you're extracting years of pattern recognition into executable form.

3. **Separate practice from evaluation**
If people think scores affect compensation, the system fails immediately. Practice must be psychologically safe. Make this explicit: "We're logging scores to track improvement, not to determine bonuses." Consider keeping practice scores private to individuals unless they choose to share.

4. **Use hiring as forcing function**
The fastest way to validate your rubrics: give candidate exercises, score them, hire based on results, then track if high-scorers actually perform better. This creates accountability—if your rubric doesn't predict success, it's wrong and needs refinement.

5. **Compound by teaching others**
Once someone masters a skill via this system, have them help refine the rubric and coach others. This creates a mentorship culture where improvement is expected and visible, not vague and heroic.

1. **Artifact-Based Skill Development Pattern**
Convert invisible thinking into visible artifacts, then create feedback loops on those artifacts. This pattern applies whenever expertise is tacit but output is concrete. Use AI's consistency and scale to provide feedback that was previously too expensive. This pattern works when: (1) quality criteria can be articulated, (2) examples exist, (3) feedback needs to be frequent and consistent, (4) human expert time is the bottleneck. 3. **Skills-Over-Jobs Career Framework Pattern**
Organize development around portable competencies (judgment, orchestration, taste) rather than role-specific duties (PM responsibilities, engineer expectations). This pattern prepares for a future where AI handles execution and humans differentiate on higher-order skills that compose across contexts.

**What to Optimize For:**

**How to Measure:**

## 9. System Health Metric

**What to Optimize For:** **Agent Work Product Quality-to-Human-Review-Time Ratio**

This measures how much high-quality work agents produce per unit of human attention spent on review and intervention. The ideal trajectory is increasing work output with decreasing human review time, indicating effective delegation, quality systems, and agent capability improvement.

**Why This Metric:** This metric captures the core transformation: we're not optimizing for "AI adoption" or "number of agents deployed" but for actual multiplication of human effectiveness. A healthy system produces more valuable output while freeing humans for higher-leverage activities. This metric directly measures whether the agent system is working as intended—amplifying human capability rather than creating new burdens.

The metric also naturally incorporates quality: if agents produce high volumes of poor work requiring extensive human cleanup, the ratio worsens. Only systems that successfully implement quality control, clear requirements, and effective evaluation harnesses will show improving ratios.

**How to Measure:**

1. **Numerator (Work Product Quality):**
   - Count outputs that pass final human review without significant revision
   - Weight by complexity/value of the work product
   - Track across different work types (code, documents, research, analysis)

2. **Denominator (Human Review Time):**
   - Measure actual human hours spent reviewing agent work
   - Include time spent unblocking agents or providing direction
   - Include time spent on quality interventions

3. **Tracking Implementation:**
   - Weekly snapshots of ratio by team/function
   - Trend line showing improvement over time
   - Comparison across different types of agent work
   - Benchmarking against baseline human-only productivity

4. **Leading Indicators:**
   - Percentage of agent outputs passing automated quality checks
   - Average agent run time before requiring human intervention
   - Human-initiated vs. agent-initiated interactions
   - Time to resolution when agents get blocked

**Target:** A healthy system shows 25-50% quarterly improvement in this ratio as agent capabilities improve, quality systems mature, and teams develop better delegation practices. Stagnation or decline indicates problems with requirements quality, evaluation systems, or agent capability.

## Strategic Patterns Identified

### 1. Power Law Divergence Pattern
Organizations split into two categories: those who completely rebuild around new technology and those who add superficial layers. The gap between categories becomes exponential over time through compound effects. This pattern appears in every major technology transition but accelerates dramatically with AI due to recursive self-improvement capabilities.

### 2. System Layer Architecture Pattern
Value comes not from any single capability but from stacking complementary layers that amplify each other: memory enables long runs, long runs enable quality checks, quality checks enable less supervision, less supervision enables more delegation, more delegation generates better training data, better training data improves all layers. The pattern is architectural—designing for layer interactions rather than optimizing individual components.

### 3. Human Attention Optimization Pattern
The fundamental constraint shifts from "what can be done" to "where should scarce human attention flow." System design becomes about minimizing human bottlenecks while maximizing human impact on high-leverage decisions. This inverts traditional automation thinking (replace humans) to attention allocation thinking (amplify humans).
