## 1. Correctness + Agent Delegation → "The Articulation Stack"
Organizations fail because they can't define "good" (correctness). Individuals fail because they can't describe "done" (articulation). **Same insight, different scales.** Train employees in articulation (individual) AND define correctness criteria (organizational) — they're the same muscle at different scales.

## 2. Tiger Teams + Side Hustle → "Internal Distribution-First"
Distribution before product + small empowered pods. **Combined:** Treat each portfolio company team like a micro-niche. Find the 3-5 person tiger team with distribution (they already use the workflow daily), build for them first, let it spread organically. Don't build for "the organization" — build for specific pods.

## 3. Second Brain + Correctness → "Clean Before You Search"
Heap-not-hierarchy (personal) vs. correctness-first (organizational). **These tension each other.** Resolution: Heap-not-hierarchy works for personal knowledge (your notes, meeting transcripts). Correctness-first applies to organizational knowledge (shared documents, official records). Don't apply the same architecture to both.

## 4. o3 Pro + Post-Knowledge Economy → "The Judgment-Model Stack"
AI crossing into strategic advising + value shifting to judgment. **Combined:** The winning stack is judgment-rich human + judgment-capable AI in a verification loop. Multi-model verification (o3 Pro → Claude Opus check → human judgment) is the operational implementation of the judgment economy.

## 5. Platform Consolidation + Agent Delegation → "Build on Platforms, Specialize in Context"
Don't build middleware platforms will absorb + context accumulation is the moat. **Combined:** Use platform-native capabilities for horizontal functions. Invest in accumulating proprietary context (domain knowledge, workflow patterns, decision history) that platforms can't replicate. Platform is the engine; your context is the fuel.

## 6. Talent Development + Tiger Teams → "The Training Paradox"
5+ hours training needed for adoption vs. small teams learn by doing. **Resolution:** Training is for the 80% (GSD users needing structured guidance). Tiger team members learn through exploration — they need permission and tools, not training programs. The mistake is applying one approach to everyone.

## 7. Davos Bottleneck + Side Hustle → "Constraint Arbitrage"
Value flows to binding constraints + exploit temporal arbitrage. **Combined:** Highest-value play = finding constraints AI has technically solved but adoption hasn't reached. For DMC: AI can do itinerary research, most competitors haven't adopted it. Constraint migrated from capability to adoption — capture value in the gap.

## 8. Agent Delegation (80% rule) + Second Brain (15-20% hallucination) → "The Imperfection Budget"
Both argue accepting imperfection is the unlock. **Combined:** Every AI system should have an explicit imperfection budget — a defined acceptable error rate calibrated against the alternative (usually: not doing it at all). Document and share the budget, don't hide it.

## 9. Post-Knowledge Economy + Correctness → "The Specification Gap"
AI-resistant skills (intent horizon, extreme agency) + humans are structurally terrible at specification. **Combined:** The most AI-resistant skill might be "precise specification under social pressure" — forcing clarity when everyone is comfortable with vagueness. This is the CEO's unique contribution: making the implicit explicit, especially when uncomfortable.

## 10. App Building + DSPy → "The Two Phases of AI Maturity"
Conversational iteration (Phase 1) + systematic optimization (Phase 2). **Not contradictory — sequential.** Phase 1 (exploration): iterate fast, embrace imperfection. Phase 2 (production): systematic optimization for reliability. The mistake: Phase 2 discipline during Phase 1 exploration, or Phase 1 messiness during Phase 2 production.
