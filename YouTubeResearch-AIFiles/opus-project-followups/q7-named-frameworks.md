## By Video Source

### o3 Pro Strategic Advisor Video

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Ferrari failure mode** | Using an overpowered model for a problem too small for its capabilities, like taking a Ferrari to the grocery store. The model over-elaborates and wastes resources. |
| **Resonance** | The meta-capability where AI outputs reorganize how you think about problems, not just provide information. Distinct from helpful, tactical, or strategic outputs. "They haven't yet been so on the money consistently that their words stick in my head and just live rent-free." |
| **Get a sandwich model** | A model that takes so long to think (15-20 minutes) that you should leave and come back. Describes o3 Pro's processing time. |
| **Strategic literacy barrier** | More powerful models require more sophisticated users, creating a widening capability divide between skilled and unskilled operators. |
| **Completeness paradox** | o3 Pro sometimes won with less complete answers because it demonstrated superior judgment about when to stop rather than exhaustively using all available capabilities. |

### AI Note-Taking / Second Brain Video

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Heap-not-hierarchy** | Throw information into an unstructured heap and let AI organize/retrieve semantically, rather than maintaining human-designed folder hierarchies. |
| **Knowledge hyperinflation** | Knowledge accumulating so fast (doubling time now in weeks/months) that traditional credentials and knowledge accumulation lose meaning, like currency in hyperinflation. |
| **Second brain** | External memory system (historical: commonplace books) that compounds in value through consistent use. Not any specific tool — the practice itself. |
| **Fool's gold detection** | The human skill of recognizing when AI output looks right but is hallucinated. The most valuable skill shifts from "organizing" to "having taste to see when the AI is wrong." |

### Agent Delegation Video (Manis, Notion AI, Lovable, Zapier)

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **"Little guy" framework** | Treating AI agents as junior employees rather than magic tools. Sets appropriate expectations: check their work, limit permissions, expand scope gradually. |
| **Progressive trust expansion loop** | Clear articulation → reliable outcome → verified success → increased trust → broader delegation → more context → better articulation. The primary flywheel for agent adoption. |
| **80% reliable rule** | 100% ambition creates 60% reliability requiring 100% verification. 80% scope with 95% reliability requires only 20% verification, delivering 5x more net value. Less capability → more value. |
| **Habitat specialization** | Start with "one little guy in one habitat" (just web research OR just workspace intelligence). Mixing habitats too early creates complexity that overwhelms before trust is established. |
| **Articulation bottleneck** | The ability to clearly describe "what done looks like" is THE competitive advantage. Technical capability is commoditizing; articulation compounds as a skill. |
| **Proof systems** | Verification mechanisms built INTO prompts (source URLs, screenshots, audit trails), not added after. Structural rather than optional verification. |

### Side Hustle / Micro-Niche Video

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Distribution-first** | Invert the traditional build sequence: Distribution → Problem → Product (not Problem → Product → Distribution). Forces market validation before creation effort. |
| **Intelligence-resistant problems** | Problems that more intelligence doesn't solve: coordination between systems/people, physical-digital bridges, workflow completion requiring approvals/stages. |
| **Software scalpel vs. hammer** | Software was historically a blunt hammer (expensive, team-required). AI makes it a scalpel — precise, cheap solutions for micro-niches. |
| **Temporal arbitrage window** | The current moment where technology is ahead of the adoption curve. Early movers capture value; window closes as adoption catches up. |

### Platform Consolidation / Claude Skills Video

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Simplicity beats infrastructure** | Natural language iteration with minimal overhead outperforms elaborate scaffolding, RAG systems, and prompt engineering frameworks. |
| **Platform consolidation thesis** | Major platforms systematically absorb middleware layers by embedding capabilities natively. Don't build what platforms will commoditize. |
| **Discovery over search** | Conversational intent replacing explicit search queries. "I want a relaxing weekend in Finland" vs. dropdown filters. |

### CTO / Correctness Video

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Correctness upstream of everything** | You cannot choose RAG vs. agents, model selection, or architecture until you answer "what would correct even mean here?" Definition precedes architecture. |
| **Vagueness as social lubricant** | Humans evolved to use vagueness to keep options open and avoid conflict. Worked for 500,000 years. Fails catastrophically with AI systems that are literal optimizers. |
| **Reward hacking** | Goodhart's Law applied to AI: systems optimize for whatever signals humans accidentally provide, leading to confidently wrong outputs that satisfy proxy metrics. |

### Tiger Teams vs. Magnifying Glass Video

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Tiger team vs. magnifying glass** | Two management philosophies for AI era. Tiger teams: small empowered pods using AI for execution. Magnifying glass: using AI for surveillance/visibility/dashboards. Tiger teams win. |
| **Visibility trap** (counter-flywheel) | Leadership adopts AI dashboard → more metrics trackable → teams optimize for metrics over outcomes → real work goes underground → leadership feels blind → adds more tracking. Doom loop. |
| **Spike mode** | Engineers given freedom to hack together solutions over weekends/evenings without permission. Produces breakthroughs that supervised work never would. |

### Post-Knowledge Economy Video

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Judgment economy** | The successor to the knowledge economy. Value shifts from knowledge accumulation to knowing what to do with infinite knowledge. |
| **Jagged intelligence frontier** | Both humans and AIs have jagged intelligence surfaces with unpredictable strengths and weaknesses. The strategic move is mapping where AI is weak and investing there. |
| **Intent horizon** | The ability to maintain coherent long-term goals. AI's context windows (3-7 hours) can't match human ability to maintain intent across weeks/months/years. |
| **Interruptability** | Graceful handling of interruptions and context switches. Humans do this naturally; AI architecturally struggles. |
| **Learning velocity** | Speed of adaptation to new information. AI is "frozen at deployment" and "instantiated and amnesiac" (Karpathy's term, cited by Nate). |

### Davos / Bottleneck Analysis Video

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Constraint migration** | Each wave of technology abundance doesn't eliminate constraints — it migrates them downstream to the next bottleneck. Winners anticipate where scarcity flows next. |
| **Integration gap** | The $4.5 trillion gap between theoretical AI capability and practical implementation. Bridging this gap is where value is captured. |
| **Atoms, not bits** | Digital transitions initially appear to transcend physical limitations, but at scale, physical constraints (energy, land, cooling) become binding again. |

### Talent Development / 201-Level Video

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **201-level gap** | The training market has bifurcated: 101-level basics and 401-level technical implementation exist. The profitable middle — 201-level applied judgment — is completely abandoned. |
| **Centaur vs. cyborg patterns** | Centaur: clearly divided human/AI work with verification checkpoints. Cyborg: fully integrated workflow. These are task-specific, not user-specific. |
| **5-hour threshold** | Employees receiving 5+ hours of structured AI training are double-digit percentage points more likely to become regular users. Most organizations offer 30 minutes. |
| **Task-mission decoupling** | Roles derive value from mission alignment, not task execution. If AI does 30% of tasks, does that bring you 30% closer to mission or hollow out the role? |
| **Execution gap** | The scarcest resource in 2024-2026 is not knowledge but execution ability — completing projects, shipping artifacts, demonstrating value. |

### ChatGPT-5 / App Building Video

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Conversational iteration** | AI development as rapid feedback with an imperfect partner, not perfect upfront specification. "Clear intent, loose specification." |
| **Time-to-usable-artifact (TtUA)** | The metric that matters: time from first prompt to something you'd actually use, including all debugging. |
| **Prompt reflection** | Asking the AI to analyze the conversation and generate an improved prompt that could reproduce the result. "The most valuable output isn't the app — it's the final prompt." |
| **10-minute working / 25-minute polished** | 90% of value in first 10 minutes, last 10% requires 15 more. New project management heuristic. |

### Perpetual Agent / Mini-Me Video

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Mini-me / perpetual agent** | An always-on agent that knows your priorities, communication style, and work patterns. Translates vague intentions into structured task lists. |
| **Delegation depth** | The complexity and abstraction level of work users successfully delegate without detailed instructions. The metric for whether the system is shifting humans from execution to strategy. |
| **Translation layer** | The component that converts messy human intentions into structured agent instructions. The missing piece in most agent architectures. |

### DSPy / Prompt Optimization Video

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Signatures** (DSPy term) | Input-output contracts that specify WHAT a module should do without dictating HOW. (e.g., "question → answer") |
| **Consistency over excellence** | DSPy scales consistently in a way no human prompter can. The advantage isn't peak performance — it's reliable floors. |
| **Three is the minimum viable pattern** | Only 3 input-output examples needed for basic pattern recognition. Lower than most would guess. |
